# Build the automation architect factories lack

Nobody sells your product yet. As of October 2026, no company takes a manufacturer's mixed inputs (photos, video, floor plans, ERP and order data, text) and returns a vendor-neutral automation roadmap for the whole facility, with equipment specs, layout, integration architecture, ROI and supply chain. The closest is **Robotiq IQ** (June 2026), which turns voice notes, files and 3D scans into validated workcell designs within about 24 hours. It covers **palletizing only**, uses Robotiq hardware and is sold through integrators. Vention designs only from its own parts catalog. Siemens, NVIDIA, Accenture and Dassault sell expert-operated digital-twin platforms to large enterprises. The best way to build it is **not** a custom-trained LLM, and it is not "Claude with a giant prompt" either. Use a multi-provider frontier LLM as the planner and orchestrator. Surround it with a structured component knowledge graph, deterministic engineering tools that produce every number (layout solvers, discrete-event simulation, robot simulators), specialist perception models for video, photos and 3D, verification loops, and sign-off by a licensed engineer. Every published comparison of domain-trained and frontier models on open-ended reasoning favored the frontier model, so train only narrow models (perception, cost and cycle-time estimators, rankers), and only once proprietary outcome data exists. For the custom-apparel beachhead, printing, curing, folding, bagging and shipping can be automated today. Picking a floppy blank and dressing it on a platen or hoop, robotic hooping, and general sewing cannot. The honest 2026 target is a **"lights-sparse" plant**: a staffed first shift plus unattended overnight windows, not zero humans. Plan-only software competes with free integrator scoping, and thin-margin SMB robot services have failed (Rapid Robotics, READY Robotics). The business therefore has to capture deployment value through design-build, financing and integrator partnerships, and should own one or two "lighthouse" factories that generate the outcome data no competitor has.

## Frontier models plus verified engineering tools beat a custom model

### The evidence runs against domain-pretrained LLMs for open-ended reasoning

Every head-to-head comparison in the research points the same way. Bloomberg trained a **50B-parameter finance model**. Within months, ChatGPT and GPT-4 beat it on nearly every financial NLP dataset; the exception was named-entity recognition, where specialists still won ([arXiv 2305.05862](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.05862)). Microsoft's Medprompt used prompting strategies alone to lift general-purpose GPT-4 past the specialist Med-PaLM 2 on all nine MultiMedQA datasets, "by a large margin with an order of magnitude fewer calls," cutting MedQA error by 27% ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/can-generalist-foundation-models-outcompete-special-purpose-tuning-case-study-in-medicine/)). For injecting knowledge, retrieval beat unsupervised fine-tuning consistently, because LLMs "struggle to learn new factual information through unsupervised fine-tuning" ([Ovadia et al., EMNLP 2024](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934?context=cs)). Supervised fine-tuning still helps, and its gains add to retrieval: **+6 points from fine-tuning plus +5 points from RAG** in Microsoft's agriculture study ([arXiv 2401.08406](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08406v2)). Harvey is the instructive vertical-AI story. It began with a custom-trained OpenAI case-law model ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harvey)) and later went multi-provider after its own benchmarks showed different models leading on different legal tasks ([Harvey](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/expanding-harveys-model-offerings)).

Specialists do win somewhere, and the line is predictable. A fine-tuned local model beat frontier models by **15–20 points** on fixed-taxonomy security classification ([arXiv 2605.20368](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20368)). A domain model topped a 2026 vulnerability benchmark with an F1 of 0.873 ([arXiv 2605.23243](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23243v3)). Domain fine-tuning can also backfire: seed-science models lost instruction-following ability and fell behind general models ([arXiv 2505.13220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13220)). The rule is that fixed schemas, fixed labels and pixels favor trained specialists, while open-ended, multi-constraint synthesis favors frontier generalists. Automation planning is mostly the second kind of task, with a few clearly bounded pockets of the first.

### The verdict: buy the reasoning, build the verifiers, train narrow models later

The right architecture is neither "our own model" nor "the Claude API with a large preset prompt." A prompt cannot hold a robot catalog, and an LLM should never compute a cycle time, reach envelope or payback in its head. Every number in the deliverable must trace to a tool run or a catalog record. The strongest evidence for this design comes from industrial code generation. **LLM4PLC** put grammar checkers, compilers and model checkers in the loop and raised PLC code generation success from **47% to 72%** and expert-rated quality from **2.25 to 7.75 out of 10** ([arXiv 2401.05443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05443v1)). That gain from verification exceeds typical fine-tuning gains, so verifiers are where the first engineering dollars should go. Siemens ships the same pattern, "an orchestrator that incorporates a toolbox of specialised agents" ([Siemens](https://news.siemens.com/en-us/ai-agents-manufacturing)). LLM agents generating Asset Administration Shell (AAS) component records reached only a **62–79% effective generation rate** ([arXiv 2403.17209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17209)), so even catalog extraction needs schema validation and human QA.

| Layer | What to use | Build, buy or train |
|---|---|---|
| Reasoning and orchestration | Frontier LLMs (Claude, GPT, Gemini) behind a model-agnostic router; separate planner and critic roles; tool calling via MCP | **Buy.** Re-run your private benchmark on each new release |
| Photo asset recognition | Open-vocabulary detector fine-tuned on your equipment image set; frontier vision model as fallback | **Train** once you have about 1K–10K labeled images per class family (heuristic) |
| Video time-and-motion | Temporal action-segmentation models (the Drishti approach), with a frontier video model for a zero-shot first pass | **Train** on consented, labeled shop video |
| 3D floor capture | Phone LiDAR, Matterport or Gaussian splats, then a mesh, then floor-plan vectorization | **Buy and integrate** |
| Layout | MIP or CP-SAT facility-layout models, metaheuristics, and a safety-distance checker | **Build** (deterministic) |
| Throughput | Discrete-event simulation: SimPy internally, with export to Plant Simulation, FlexSim or AnyLogic | **Build** |
| Robot reach and cycle time | RoboDK or vendor offline-programming tools; Isaac Sim or MuJoCo for physics and synthetic data | **Buy and integrate** |
| Custom fixtures and guards | LLM-written code-CAD (CadQuery or build123d) executed in a CAD kernel; parametric templates | **Build.** Avoid multi-body text-to-CAD |
| Cost, BOM and ROI | Deterministic calculators now; gradient-boosted estimators later | **Train** after about 100–500 labeled projects per cell family (heuristic) |
| Datasheet-to-catalog extraction | Frontier extraction, later distilled into a small open model | **Distill** (fixed schema favors fine-tuning) |

Text-to-CAD illustrates where the technology stands. Code-based CAD lets models reuse their coding skill and improves validity and geometric accuracy ([arXiv 2605.18430](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18430)). But the 2026 MUSE benchmark finds that "multi-component spatial reasoning is a major bottleneck" ([arXiv 2605.28579](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.28579)). Use vendor STEP files and parametric templates for cells, and limit generated geometry to simple brackets, fixtures and guards.

Do not build a physical-AI foundation model either. NVIDIA releases open vision-language-action models (GR00T N1.7 is a 3B-parameter model pretrained on more than 20,000 hours of egocentric video, per secondary coverage), and ABB, FANUC, KUKA and Yaskawa all announced Omniverse and Isaac integration for virtual commissioning at GTC 2026 ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-and-global-robotics-leaders-take-physical-ai-to-the-real-world); [A3](https://www.automate.org/ai/industry-insights/nvidia-declares-big-bang-of-physical-ai-at-gtc-2026)). Use OpenUSD and Omniverse as your 3D validation substrate, use world models for synthetic perception training data, and list VLA-driven "flexible cells" in your catalog with honest maturity ratings.

### When training your own model becomes worth it

Post-training the core planner early repeats BloombergGPT's mistake: the next frontier release, typically three to six months away, erases the gain. The moat is a data loop, not weights. Rank your proprietary assets in this order: predicted versus actual cycle time, uptime, cost and ROI for every deployed cell; engineer edits and accept/reject decisions on AI drafts, which become preference data; labeled factory video and photos; real quotes, prices and lead times; and a library of validated cell designs. Use them first as retrieval and as a private "Factory Automation Bench," since no public automation-planning benchmark exists. Then train narrow models. Consider reinforcement fine-tuning a planner only once an automatic grader exists (simulation pass/fail plus constraint satisfaction plus engineer score) and frontier prompting has visibly plateaued on your benchmark. Fine-tuning access is uneven. OpenAI's grader-based reinforcement fine-tuning became generally available around May 2025 ([OpenAI](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/rft-use-cases)). The only Claude fine-tuning found was for Claude 3 Haiku on Bedrock ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2024/11/fine-tuning-anthropics-claude-3-haiku-amazon-bedrock)). Open-weight models fine-tuned in your own VPC are the fallback when control matters.

Data sensitivity shapes the stack as much as accuracy does. Customer CAD and process video are trade secrets. As of August 2026, all four major providers offer zero data retention, but none enables it by default and each uses a different mechanism ([wecallshotgun](https://wecallshotgun.com/blog/zero-data-retention-ai-models-comparison), secondary). Anthropic's "covered models" policy, effective June 9, 2026, retains prompts and outputs for its most capable tier for **30 days on every platform**, overriding existing zero-retention agreements ([Anthropic](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15425695-covered-models)). The design response is a sensitivity router. Raw video and CAD go to in-VPC perception models and open-weight LLMs. Abstracted structured specs (cycle times, part envelopes, takt) go to the strongest frontier model. Retention-bound models are used only where the customer contract allows it. Deploying through Bedrock, Vertex, Azure and self-hosted models keeps defense, pharma and automotive customers reachable.

## Custom apparel can reach lights-sparse operation, not true lights-out

### Printing is solved; dressing a floppy garment is not

The beachhead teaches the product's hardest lesson early: in soft goods, the bottleneck is handling the material, not running the process. Kornit's **Apollo** prints up to **400 unique garments per hour** with integrated curing and **automatic unloading**, but loading is semi-automatic, meaning "the operator hand[s] the garment to the semi-automatic loader" ([Kornit brochure](https://www.kornit.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Apollo_brochure_15-Oct-23_DIGITAL_V06.pdf); [Impressions](https://impressionsmagazine.com/news/kornit-digital-unveils-new-apollo-platform-and-enhanced-atlas-max-plus/39113/)). M&R's Passport unloads screen-printed shirts at up to **100 dozen per hour** and offers no automatic loader ([M&R](https://www.mrprint.com/equipment/passport-automatic-t-shirt-unloader)). DTF is the most automation-friendly print path because printing happens on roll film: a Roland TY-300 with shaker runs roll-to-roll "for unattended production" for **$28,995** ([Airmark](https://www.airmark.com/products/roland-ty-300-30-dtf-printer-and-rdtfs800-shaker-bundle)). That moves the hard step to heat-press placement. Embroidery machines handle color changes, trims and thread tension automatically, but no commercial robotic hooping system was found; magnetic hoops are the practical improvement ([MagneticHoop](https://www.magnetichoop.com/blogs/news/zsk-vs-tajima-2025-expert-analysis-for-commercial-embroidery-success)). Fold-bag-seal machines cost **$4K–$30K** and claim **400–500 pieces per hour**, but a human still lays each shirt flat ([Made-in-China/Beyao](https://beyaomachinery.en.made-in-china.com/product/DQZpeaPxJBcu/China-Automatic-Garment-Apparel-Clothes-T-Shirt-Folding-Bagging-Machine.html); [EcoFreen](https://www.ecofreen.us/products/dtf-station-tee-folder-pro-automatic-shirt-folding-and-bagging-machine)).

AI cloth handling is advancing, but not fast enough to dress platens yet. Dyna Robotics showed bimanual folding with **>99% internal success**, with robots in hotels and laundries running up to 16 hours a day ([heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Dyna-robot-folds-laundry-by-itself-11133562.html)). Folding became the industry demo precisely because "we basically couldn't do this before" ([Paxton](https://itcanthink.substack.com/p/why-is-everyones-robot-folding-clothes)). Humanoids are in paid pilots moving totes, not garments, and pilots for garment loading look like a **2027–2028** prospect. Sewing remains the deepest hole. SoftWear Automation raised a **$20M Series B1 in August 2025** and claims its third-generation T-shirt Sewbot makes a shirt every 22 seconds at $0.33 of labor. Those are company claims reported secondhand, and the company is still pre-scale more than a decade after founding ([SoftWear](https://softwearautomation.com/softwear-automation-secures-20-million-in-series-b1-funding-round-led-by-strategic-partnership-with-bestseller/); [RetailBoss](https://retailboss.co/sewbot-technology-drives-softwear-automation-bestseller-20-million-dollar-apparel-revolution/)).

### The near-lights-out target and why it is deliberately limited

The plan your product should generate for a blanks-based custom-apparel or print-on-demand shop looks like this. Software automates order intake, art checks, imposition and routing. AMRs deliver **pre-folded, flat blanks in a known orientation** by SKU and size. That one workaround turns an unsolved cloth-singulation problem into a solvable flat-stock pick. One loader per DTG line feeds an Apollo-class printer that prints, cures and unloads automatically. DTF film runs unattended overnight, and already-hooped embroidery runs finish without staff. Vision QC sends defects to a reject lane, fold-bag-seal machines and auto-labeling handle packout, and a small roaming team handles exceptions. Touches per garment fall from many to roughly one or two. Even showcase plants keep people. FANUC has run lights-out since 2001, with robots building robots unsupervised for up to 30 days, but on a low-mix, engineered product ([Wikipedia: Lights out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lights_out_(manufacturing))). Siemens Amberg automates about **75% of its value chain** ([Develop3D](https://develop3d.com/develop3d-blog/the-internet-of-things-iot-and-automation-in-the-smart-factory/)). Xiaomi's factory keeps a supervisory "war room" ([New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/robotics/xiaomi-dark-robotic-factory/)). Adidas closed its Speedfactories in 2019–2020 because automation could not overcome cost, scale and supply-chain realities ([Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/adidas-speedfactory-blunder-distributed-operations/571678/)). Tesla's "excessive automation… was a mistake" is the other canonical warning ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/04/13/elon-musk-says-humans-are-underrated-calls-teslas-excessive-automation-a-mistake/)). The product's default rules should therefore be: automate the bottleneck first, keep humans at variable soft-goods touchpoints, size the plan for the actual SKU mix, and treat lights-out as a staged end state.

## The execution blueprint, part one: product, AI stack, data and integration

### The deliverable: a decision-grade plan with provenance for every number

Sell a **Dark Factory Roadmap** with three tiers of fidelity. Each tier is a superset of the one before, and each converts into the next.

| Tier | Contents | Fidelity | Recommended price (opinion) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Automation Readiness Scan | Readiness score (SIRI or acatech stage), VSM from order data, top-10 automation opportunities ranked by payback, budgetary capex range | ±30–50% cost; no site visit | Free to low four figures (lead generation) |
| 2. Dark Factory Roadmap | Everything in Tier 1 plus current-state twin; phased target state; equipment selection with alternates; facility layout; throughput simulation with ramp curve; integration-tier rating per machine; controls/UNS architecture; preliminary risk assessment; BOM with landed cost and lead times; after-tax ROI with capex vs. lease vs. RaaS vs. click-pricing options; residual staffing plan; supply-chain plan | ±15–25%; remote capture plus one engineer review | Roughly $10K–$40K per facility, credited toward a build |
| 3. Engineering & RFP Package | Cell-level designs, AutomationML/AAS/STEP/OpenUSD exports, SimPy or commercial simulation models, PLC/PackML scope, safety concept, integrator RFP, FAT/SAT plan | Engineer-stamped where law requires; laser-surveyed | Fixed fee or folded into design-build margin |

Three properties set this apart from a consultant's deck. Every cycle time, reach, footprint and dollar figure links to the tool run or dated catalog record behind it. Uncertainty is shown as ranges, not point estimates. And the plan exports into the tools downstream engineers already use, such as Siemens Eigen, Rockwell Copilot, Vention or Robotiq, so it feeds those ecosystems rather than fighting them. The ROI section should model after-tax cash flows. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act made **100% bonus depreciation permanent** for qualifying property acquired after January 19, 2025, and raised Section 179 expensing to **$2.5M** ([BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-expands-100-depreciation-expensing-opportunities)). For an SMB owner, that is often the decisive sales argument. Model a ramp-up curve rather than nameplate output, and capture the value of second and third shifts, which is where lights-out economics live.

### Reference AI architecture: six layers around a plant model

The core data object is a structured **Plant Model**: a graph or JSON source of truth aligned to AAS and AutomationML. Everything reads from and writes to it. The **ingestion and perception layer**, deployable inside the customer's VPC, parses documents and ERP/e-commerce exports, detects assets in photos, segments operator actions in video, converts 3D scans into floor plans, and reads DXF, STEP and IFC files. The **knowledge layer** holds the component graph, a vector index over manuals and past projects, and validated cell templates. The **reasoning layer** runs frontier LLMs with a planner/critic split (preferably from different providers), long-context project dossiers and tool calls. The **engineering tool layer** produces every number: layout optimizer, discrete-event simulation, robot simulation, code-CAD, safety-distance checker and BOM/ROI calculator. The **verification layer** checks hard constraints (payload, reach, takt, footprint, utilities, ISO 13855 distances), feeds violations back to the planner, runs cross-model critique, and ends in a human automation engineer's sign-off. The **learning layer** logs every draft, edit and real-world outcome into the benchmark and training sets. The loop is: ingest, propose candidates via retrieval, simulate and solve, verify, critique, get human sign-off, then log outcomes.

### Data intake spec: measure distributions, not averages

The intake should mirror what integrators collect during discovery: personnel interviews, IT/OT risk assessment, equipment mapping and input from every department ([Control Engineering](https://www.controleng.com/best-practices-for-effective-automation-applications-part-1-automation-effectiveness-through-data-collection)). Wherever possible, capture it automatically rather than by questionnaire. The biggest planning risk is variability. Mean cycle times hide the long-tail SKUs and exceptions that break unattended operation, so the intake must capture distributions and the exception log.

| Data domain | What to collect | Best capture method | Accuracy and notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand | 12–24 months of line-item orders; SKU × color × size × decoration × location; order-size distribution; seasonality and peak-week multiplier; SLAs; margin by order class | Shopify Orders API; DecoNetwork, Printavo or ShopVOX connectors | Most reliable automatic source; build this connector first |
| Process | VSM with WIP queues; cycle-time distribution per step; changeovers; first-pass yield and defect Pareto; rework loops; labor hours per step and shift | Video action recognition; clamp-on current sensors joined to order timestamps; MOST synthetic times as fallback | Video works best at fixed repetitive stations; synthetic times carry about ±10–20% uncertainty |
| Equipment | Make, model, serial and year; control interface (none, I/O, serial, Ethernet, OPC UA, vendor API, hot folder); job-input method; MTBF/MTTR; consumables | Photo detection reconciled with a guided checklist; nameplate photos | Drives the integration-tier rating |
| Facility | Dimensioned floor plan; column grid; doors; clear height; floor flatness and load rating; power; compressed air; HVAC and humidity; exhaust; Wi-Fi; fire code | Phone LiDAR or Matterport; utility photos | Phone LiDAR is about ±1.3 cm, fine for concepts; laser-survey before final cell design |
| IT/OT | E-commerce, OMS, ERP, shipping software; API keys; artwork formats | OAuth connectors | API depth of shop software is poorly documented |
| Quality and safety | Inspection criteria; color tolerance; return reasons; OSHA log; SDSs for pretreat and inks; insurance constraints | Document upload | — |
| Finance and strategy | Budget; payback hurdle; fully burdened labor cost; turnover; appetite for lease or RaaS; tasks that must stay human | Structured interview | Determines which plan variant to present |

Polycam states ±½-inch accuracy on standard interiors ([Polycam](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/50434745985556-How-Accurate-Are-Polycam-Scans)). Clamp-on current sensors give run/idle/off state within hours without touching controls ([Fabrico](https://www.fabrico.io/blog/best-oee-software-legacy-equipment-retrofit-2026-review)). Drishti-style action recognition turns station video into cycle data ([IndustryWeek](https://www.industryweek.com/technology-and-iiot/article/21124036/ai-based-action-recognition-technology-reaps-rewards)). Ship a capture kit (sensors, a phone-scan app and camera mounts) so the customer can gather a two-week measured baseline before the roadmap is written. Video capture needs worker consent and a privacy policy from day one.

### Integration reference architecture: a Unified Namespace over standard machine models

Every plan should prescribe the same integration backbone so that a patchwork of manual stations becomes one coordinated system. ISA-95 provides the semantic hierarchy (enterprise, site, area, line, cell, device). A **Unified Namespace** of MQTT plus Sparkplug B, now **ISO/IEC 20237**, replaces point-to-point integrations with a hub that every system publishes to and subscribes from ([Eclipse Foundation](https://newsroom.eclipse.org/node/39754); [Litmus](https://litmus.io/blog/how-to-build-a-unified-namespace-architecture-timeline-and-failure-modes)). Below the UNS, each machine exposes a PackML state model (ANSI/ISA-TR88.00.02-2022) over OPC UA or through a gateway ([Control Engineering](https://www.controleng.com/articles/updated-packml-standard-released/)). Robots use the OPC UA Robotics companion spec (VDMA 40010) and vision systems use VDMA 40100 ([OPC Foundation](https://opcfoundation.org/news/press-releases/vdma-releases-opc-ua-companion-specifications-robotics-machine-vision/)). Mixed AMR fleets run under one master controller via **VDA 5050 v3.0.0**, adopted in February 2026 and now covering AMRs ([idealworks](https://idealworks.com/en/news/vda5050-300-release-march-2026)). Use the UNS for events and state, and request/response calls for commands that need acknowledgment, such as job dispatch. Litmus puts a UNS rollout at **3–12 months, typically 6–9**, and names the failure modes to design against: missing naming governance, scope creep, treating the UNS as a replacement for SCADA or MES, weak network segmentation, and part-time staffing.

| Level | Components | Plan output |
|---|---|---|
| L4 Enterprise | Shopify / web-to-print, ERP, accounting, shipping | Connectors and webhooks |
| L3 Operations | OMS (KornitX, DecoNetwork), MES/orchestrator, WMS, AMR fleet manager | Job-ticket schema (barcode/RFID: blank SKU, art file, placement, finishing) |
| Edge | DataOps gateway (Ignition Edge, HighByte, Litmus class); MQTT/Sparkplug B broker | ISA-95 topic tree; naming governance |
| L2 Control | Cell PLCs (IEC 61131-3), safety PLCs, robot controllers | PackML states, including Held/Stopped "safe park" states |
| L0–L1 Devices | Printers, dryers, presses, embroidery heads, robots, scanners, sensors | Integration-tier rating and adapter spec per machine |

Most apparel-decoration equipment has **no OPC UA companion spec**. Its integration surface is usually a RIP hot folder or a vendor cloud, so each machine gets an integration-tier rating: native API or OPC UA; vendor cloud or hot folder; I/O only; monitoring only; replace. That rating drives the retrofit-versus-replace capex split. Monitoring retrofits provide observability but no control. A dark plant needs a command path for every machine: remote start, a job-file path, and done/fault signals. Having a cobot press HMI buttons is a last resort and should be flagged high-risk. The order-to-machine flow runs: order webhook, OMS, orchestrator creates the job ticket, WMS picks the blank, AMR delivers it, the cell loads and scans, the RIP or embroidery file is fetched by job ID, then print, cure, unload, vision QC, fold and bag, label, ship, with every state change published to the UNS. KornitX already handles "pixel to parcel" order routing for Kornit lines ([KornitX](https://www.amayauk.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/KornitX-The-Six-Stages-of-On-Demand-Workflow-September-2021.pdf)), so your orchestrator should integrate with it rather than replace it. Lights-out readiness also requires remote alarming with on-call escalation, condition-based maintenance, bulk ink and pretreat feeds sized to the unattended window, quarantine buffers so one fault does not stop the line, fire and thermal monitoring for unattended dryers, and OT network segmentation. Commissioning should run through simulation and virtual commissioning before FAT and SAT. Vendor case studies report **40–70% shorter on-site commissioning** with digital twins ([Siemens/Wipro PARI](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/tecnomatix/wipro-pari-reduces-commissioning-time-by-70/); [Kalypso](https://kalypso.com/viewpoints/entry/reducing-commissioning-time-by-40-with-a-digital-twin)).

### Knowledge base and supply-chain module

No single feed covers automation hardware, so the knowledge base is a licensed composite. Use a typed component graph keyed to ECLASS properties and AAS submodels (IDTA 02003 Technical Data; IDTA 02006 Digital Nameplate v3.0.1) ([IDTA](https://industrialdigitaltwin.org/en/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/10/IDTA-02006-3-0-1_Submodel_Digital-Nameplate.pdf)), with compatibility, requirement and substitution edges, and linked STEP and URDF geometry. Selection is mostly numeric constraint filtering, so query the graph or SQL first and use vector search only for manuals and past projects. Feed it from distributor APIs (Digi-Key's default is about 1,000 calls per day; Nexar starts at 1,000 parts per month; Mouser), McMaster's approved-customer API, MISUMI punchout, TraceParts (customers own the CAD they export), and Vention's live-priced BOMs ([Digi-Key](https://developer.digikey.com/documentation); [Nexar](https://support.nexar.com/support/solutions/articles/101000497890-frequently-asked-questions); [TraceParts](https://info.traceparts.com/legal/pcs-terms-and-conditions-for-parts-distributors); [Vention](https://vention.io/resources/faqs)). License the data rather than scraping it, and get legal review on redistributing cached prices.

Robot OEMs do not publish list prices, so store every price as a dated range with a confidence tier. Secondary sources put a UR5e at **$30K–$45K**, roughly $60K once tooled ([Standard Bots](https://standardbots.com/blog/universal-robot-price)), and FANUC CRX at **$40K–$65K** ([Standard Bots](https://standardbots.com/blog/fanuc-cobot-price)). Note that Standard Bots sells a competing cobot. A3 order data implies about **$62K per robot** in H1 2025 ([Plant Automation Technology](https://www.plantautomation-technology.com/index.php/news/new-a3-report-reveals-north-american-robot-orders-rise-in-h1-2025-signaling-continued-industrial-automation-investment)). Installed cost follows from hardware: the arm is about **25–40%** of the cell ([AMD Machines](https://amdmachines.com/blog/total-cost-of-ownership-for-robotic-systems/)). Apply an integration multiplier of about 1.5–2.0× for first-of-kind cells and 1.2–1.4× for repeat modular cells, then recalibrate against your own quotes and actual costs.

Supply risk must be part of the plan. Average component lead times rose to **20.6 weeks** in 2026 ([IBS Electronics](https://www.ibselectronics.com/resources/blog/industrial-component-supply-comes-under-pressure-in-2026/)), and resellers report **26–52 weeks** for some Allen-Bradley and Siemens PLC components; that figure is anecdotal ([Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/plc-component-supply-chain-shortages-lead-times-alternatives)). Chinese-origin robots pay an additional **25% Section 301 duty**, and some exclusions run to November 10, 2026 ([USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2025/november/ustr-extends-exclusions-china-section-301-tariffs-related-forced-technology-transfer-investigation); [SourceBotics](https://sourcebotics.com/guides/us-tariffs-on-chinese-robots-2026/)). Since April 2026, Section 232 metal-derivative duties apply to full customs value ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/04/current-and-forthcoming-section-232-actions-by-the-trump-administration)). The Section 232 robotics and machinery investigation had no announced outcome as of July 2026, and its October status is unconfirmed ([ManufacturingMag](https://www.manufacturingmag.com/article/section-232-june-2026-machinery-tariffs-imported-automation-reshoring-paradox)). One aggregator's "25–145%" figure for Chinese robotics tariffs matches the April 2025 peak and should be ignored. Each plan should therefore compute landed cost by HTS code and origin, flag anything over 12 weeks as critical-path, default to second-source PLC families, and pre-buy long-lead control hardware at contract signing.

The operational supply-chain module is unusually tractable in apparel. The **S&S Activewear API** covers inventory (refreshed every 15 minutes), orders and tracking at 60 calls per minute ([S&S](https://api.ssactivewear.com/V2/Default.aspx)). SanMar exposes PromoStandards inventory and pricing services ([SanMar](https://www.sanmar.com/medias/sys_master/root/h5b/hde/10127344533534/SanMar-Web-Services-Integration-Guide-v18.8.pdf?attachment=true)). PSRESTful wraps **600+ PromoStandards suppliers** in one REST API ([PSRESTful](https://psrestful.com/)). The module can rank suppliers by transit days, price breaks and stock depth, place POs, compute reorder points for inks, film and powder (which have no APIs), and route overflow or out-of-capability SKUs to Printful through Shopify order routing ([Printful](https://help.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/8110629994268-How-do-I-set-up-Printful-as-a-backup-fulfillment-facility)). The redesign should also feed lights-out cells: kitting, pre-folded flat blanks, and buffers sized to the unattended window. Hand ongoing tail-spend procurement to agents like Didero ($30M Series A, February 2026) through partnership rather than competing with them ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/02/20/didero-30-million-funding-ai-procurement/)).

## The execution blueprint, part two: business model, go-to-market, liability, team and roadmap

### Business model: a planning engine that earns money on deployment

Plan fees alone will not build a large company. The SMB base is large: **239K US manufacturers, 74% with fewer than 20 employees** ([NAM](https://nam.org/facts-about-manufacturing/)), and robot adoption is concentrated in large firms ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/books-and-chapters/technology-productivity-and-economic-growth/automation-and-workforce-firm-level-view-2019-annual-business-survey)). But integrators give scoping away to win builds, and thin-margin SMB robot services have struggled. Rapid Robotics raised about $54M and ended in an asset sale to RobCo in September 2025 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250909601076/en/RobCo-Launches-in-the-US-With-New-San-Francisco-HQ-to-Meet-Soaring-Automation-Demand-Amid-Industry-Reshoring-Drive-and-Labor-Shortage)). READY Robotics shut down after $41.5M ([robotics.press](https://robotics.press/news/ready-robotics-shuts-down-after-raising-41-5m-in/)). Custom apparel is a wedge, not the end market: about **$8.7B in US decorated apparel** in 2025, per a single low-reliability estimate ([Nova One Advisor](https://www.novaoneadvisor.com/report/us-decorated-apparel-market)).

The recommended model is a four-stage funnel. A **free or cheap readiness scan** generates leads. A **paid roadmap** is credited toward the build. **Design-build delivery** runs through a vetted integrator network (referral fee or margin share) or an in-house deployment team, alongside **origination of financing, leases or RaaS**. A recurring **orchestration and monitoring subscription** follows once the UNS and MES layer is live. Add one or two **company-owned lighthouse factories**: acquire or build custom-apparel shops and run them on your own playbook. They prove the economics, produce the predicted-versus-actual outcome data that is your real moat, and, if the playbook works, open a buy-and-automate roll-up option in the style of General Catalyst's roughly $1.5B AI roll-up strategy ([Capital & Clarity](https://capitalandclarity.substack.com/p/the-general-catalyst-behind-15-billion)). That strategy has so far targeted services, not physical plants, so treat the roll-up as an option, not the base case. Equipment vendors are moving to usage pricing, and Kornit's Apollo sells under an "All-Inclusive Click" model ([Large Format Review](https://www.largeformatreview.com/hardware/textile-printing/monster-digital-to-add-10-kornit-apollo-systems-during-2025/)), so plans should compare capex, lease, RaaS and per-click options side by side.

### Go-to-market: enter through the equipment channel and integrators

Pick design partners among mid-size decorators and POD fulfillers ($5M–$50M revenue) already running Kornit or M&R automatics and feeling labor pressure. Printful alone runs 86 Kornit Atlas MAX systems, which shows the shape of the upper segment ([Printful](https://www.printful.com/news/upgrades-kornit-digital)). Enter through **Kornit and M&R sales channels**, whose reps gain when plans specify their equipment; through PRINTING United and Impressions Expo; and through **NIST MEP centers** in every state, which can co-deliver assessments and point shops to grants ([NIST MEP](https://www.nist.gov/mep)). Treat the roughly **3,000 US independent integrators** as delivery partners ([CSIA](https://controlsys.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/CSIA-2025-Prospectus-FNL.pdf)). Handing them pre-engineered, pre-qualified projects removes their biggest cost, the unpaid pre-sale engineering on lost bids. OEM integrator programs such as FANUC's are vetted networks, not open directories ([FANUC America](https://www.fanucamerica.com/integrators)), so recruit integrators individually. Avoid leading with "dark factory" branding. The term now mostly refers to autonomous AI software engineering ([OpenHands](https://www.openhands.dev/blog/what-is-an-ai-dark-factory)), and "right-sized automation" earns more trust from cautious owners. After apparel, expand into adjacent high-mix, low-volume light manufacturing (promotional products, signage, packaging, kitting, light assembly), where the same handling, orchestration and supply-chain modules carry over.

### Liability and safety: preliminary by AI, final by licensed engineers

US law puts application risk assessment and safeguarding on the integrator, and designs carry a PE stamp where state law requires ([ISHN](https://www.ishn.com/articles/107815-ansiria-r1506-2012--the-industrial-robot-safety-standard); [OSHA Technical Manual](https://www.osha.gov/otm/section-4-safety-hazards/chapter-4)). OSHA has no robot-specific standard and enforces 29 CFR 1910.212 machine guarding plus the General Duty Clause ([OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/interlinking/standards/1910.212(a)/standard_interpretations)). The standards shifted in 2025. **ISO 10218-1/-2:2025** absorbed ISO/TS 15066, made "collaborative" a property of the application rather than the robot, and added cybersecurity requirements ([A3](https://www.automate.org/robotics/news/updated-iso-10218-major-advancements-in-industrial-robot-safety-standards-now-available/aph)). A3 has announced a revised R15.06 aligned with it; the details were not confirmed in this research ([A3](https://www.automate.org/robotics/industry-insights/ansi-a3-publish-revised-r15-06-industrial-robot-safety-standard/aph)). The EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 applies from **January 20, 2027**, with no transition period and new software, AI and cybersecurity requirements ([TÜV Nord](https://www.tuev-nord.de/en/services/audit-and-expertise/product-certification/machinery-regulation/)).

The workable structure has four parts. Tier 1 and Tier 2 outputs are labeled "preliminary — not for construction." Every plan includes a standard safety section: an ISO 12100 risk assessment outline, target performance levels under ISO 13849-1, ISO 13855 separation distances, R15.08 for mobile robots, NFPA 79 and UL 508A for electrical, and lockout/tagout. Tier 3 designs are signed by in-house or partner PEs, with an engineering firm of record in each state of operation. The company carries professional E&O and products/completed-operations coverage. Lights-out operation does not remove safety exposure, because maintenance, restocking and fault recovery bring people into cells, and those tasks dominate the risk assessment.

### Team: integrators and engineers first, ML second

The team should reflect where the hard problems are. Hire controls and automation engineers with integrator backgrounds (FANUC- and UR-certified), industrial engineers fluent in discrete-event simulation, layout and lean, a functional-safety lead and licensed PEs. Add simulation and robotics-software engineers (Isaac, RoboDK, OPC UA, MQTT), an ML and agent-engineering group that owns the orchestration, evals and perception models, and a knowledge-base team for data partnerships, extraction QA and pricing. Bring in apparel domain operators (former Kornit, M&R or POD plant managers), a field-capture and deployment crew, and an integrator-network and channel lead. Add an M&A and plant-operations bench if the lighthouse or roll-up path is pursued. The Deloitte/Manufacturing Institute study notes a **75% rise in demand for simulation skills** over five years ([Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/manufacturing-labor-shortage-2033-deloitte-mi-report-2024/713133/)). That is both a hiring challenge and evidence that your product addresses a real shortage.

### Metrics that prove the plan works

Track predicted-versus-actual error on capex, cycle time and payback (the master metric); the share of numbers in a plan with tool or catalog provenance (target 100%); engineer hours and calendar days per roadmap (target days, not the incumbent 2–8 weeks); scan-to-roadmap and roadmap-to-build conversion; customer payback realized against the promised payback; unattended hours per week achieved in deployed plants; private benchmark score after each new frontier model release; gross margin by revenue line; and integrator partner NPS.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "custom model or Claude API?" to "where does engineering truth come from?" In this domain, truth comes from simulators, solvers, dated catalogs and measured outcomes. The LLM's job is to turn messy reality into a structured plant model and to coordinate the tools that produce the answers. A well-funded team that spends its first year on verifiers, the component graph and capture tooling will outperform one that spends it pretraining a manufacturing LLM, and it will benefit automatically from every frontier release instead of being overtaken by it. The lasting asset is the loop: predicted-versus-actual outcomes from real deployments, which no model vendor, integrator or equipment OEM is currently collecting across vendors.

The beachhead also makes a strategic point. Custom apparel has software-defined front ends and nearly automated print engines, but material handling is unsolved. That makes it an ideal proving ground for a planner whose main value is deciding *where not* to automate yet, while staging the plant so each new handling breakthrough (bimanual folding today, platen loading perhaps in 2027–2028) slots into an existing integration backbone. The company that owns the architecture, data and integrator network when those robots mature will be the one that turns broken-up manual shops into working dark factories.
