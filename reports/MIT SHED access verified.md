# Email the SHED, machine metal elsewhere meanwhile

**Short answer:** You get into the MIT SHED by **emailing SHED@mit.edu**. The SHED's "Become a User" button is just that email link, and the SHED publishes no form, training list, eligibility rules, fees or hours ([shed.mit.edu](https://shed.mit.edu/)). **You cannot use its machines whenever you want.** No hours, card-access policy or reservation system is published anywhere. The SHED sits inside the EHS office suite on the 4th floor of N52. Its public calendar has posted **no events at all since February 28, 2025**, and none are scheduled through December 2026 ([LibCal "EHS The SHED"](https://project-manus.libcal.com/calendar/shed)). On paper, its machine list is among the strongest on campus for metal work: **metal 3D printers, 4-axis and 5-axis CNC mills, lathes, waterjets and wire EDM**, kept in a sub-space called the "Fabrication Node … for machining, fabrication and metal works" ([SHED Capabilities](https://shed.mit.edu/page/capabilities)). **No public source names a single make or model**, though, and the only SHED tools students are verified to have run themselves are a laser cutter, a 3D printer and soldering gear. For CNC you can count on this term, the **Hobby Shop is free for students in 2026-27** and has a Haas Mini Mill 2, ProtoTRAK mill and lathe, and a waterjet; its last fall orientation is **Friday, October 23**. The **Edgerton 6C Student Shop** has a Haas CNC mill and lathe after 12 hours of training. **MIT has no campus metal 3D printing service.** In practice, a metal printed part means an outside DMLS vendor or a research connection to the faculty-run APT machines.

*How to read this report:* **Verified** means the claim was read on a full, current official page on 2026-10-01. **Inferred** means it is reasoning from those pages and is not stated anywhere. Everything about who may run the SHED's metal machines is inferred, because the SHED does not publish it.

## One email address is the only published way into the SHED

The SHED (Safety Health Environmental Discovery lab) is a research and prototyping lab **run by MIT Environment, Health & Safety**. It describes itself as "a multidisciplinary laboratory dedicated to prototyping, fabrication, and scientific discovery at MIT" ([About the SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/about-the-shed)). Its founding director and PI is Tolga Durak. It has four research areas: chemistry, biology up to BSL-2+, radiation and nuclear, and prototyping and fabrication. These are spread over five named sub-spaces: Life Sciences Hub, Fabrication Node, Hygieneyx, Beamworks and The Gray ([Capabilities](https://shed.mit.edu/page/capabilities)). To reach it, enter N52 from the **265 Mass Ave / Front St entrance**, go to the 4th floor, enter the EHS office and lab area, and walk straight through to the SHED ([LibCal event 13637298](https://project-manus.libcal.com/event/13637298)). The campus maker directory lists it as **"The SHED at EHS," room N52-496** ([make.mit.edu/makerspaces](https://make.mit.edu/makerspaces)).

On eligibility, the SHED says only that "we offer access to MIT faculty, staff, researchers, students, and thought leaders." The buttons under that sentence ("Become a User," "Join the Network") open an email to **SHED@mit.edu** ([shed.mit.edu](https://shed.mit.edu/)). That is all the SHED publishes about access. The site's sitemap has **no trainings, FAQ, hours, policies or contact page**, and URLs like `/faq` and `/contact` return 404 ([sitemap](https://shed.mit.edu/sitemap.xml)). The SHED defines its users as people "leveraging SHED's resources and expertise to achieve their research and innovation goals" ([About the SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/about-the-shed)). *Inferred:* access is granted case by case for a defined project. It is not a drop-in membership, so your email should lead with what you want to make and why.

The one documented student pathway has gone quiet. From December 2023 to February 2025, first-years could take a **4-hour alternative version of M1T at the SHED**, building a Lixie Clock or a Zoetrope. It earned the usual M1T credit plus "**novice laser cutter and 3D printing credentials**" ([LibCal event 13637298](https://project-manus.libcal.com/event/13637298); [LibCal event 14125842](https://project-manus.libcal.com/event/14125842)). A day-by-day sweep of the SHED's calendar from September 2023 to December 2026 found **100 events, all of them M1T device sessions, with the last on February 28, 2025**. Its iCal feed is empty ([LibCal "EHS The SHED"](https://project-manus.libcal.com/calendar/shed)). None of the 737 fall-2026 events across all MIT Making calendars takes place at the SHED or N52. First-years this fall should take standard M1T at Metropolis instead ([make.mit.edu/m1t](https://make.mit.edu/m1t)).

Credential tracking has also moved. **Mobius is retired**: mobius.mit.edu redirects to **make.mit.edu**, which now serves as the campus hub for spaces, equipment, calendars, payments and credentials ([make.mit.edu](https://make.mit.edu/); [Project Manus archive](https://project-manus.mit.edu/mobius/overview.html)). *Inferred:* after you email, expect EHS-assigned online safety training matched to the hazards you will work with (shop, laser and so on), then a hands-on checkout with a SHED mentor or staff member. The SHED says its 34 listed student and researcher mentors handle "designing trainings to hosting events and open hours" ([About the SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/about-the-shed)), but it names no specific courses.

## No posted hours means no drop-in use, let alone 24/7

The SHED mentions that mentors host "events and open hours" ([About the SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/about-the-shed)). However, **no open-hours session has ever appeared on its calendar**, and no page gives hours, card-access rules, buddy rules or a booking system ([LibCal "EHS The SHED"](https://project-manus.libcal.com/calendar/shed)). *Inferred:* the space shares a suite with EHS offices and contains BSL-2+ biology, radioactive sources, irradiators and femtosecond lasers. Use is almost certainly arranged by appointment or by project with staff or mentors, not through unsupervised card access. Plan on scheduled time, not free use.

This fits the rest of campus. Even shops with published rules restrict their high-end metal machines. Edgerton's Area 51, for example, requires at least one other person present, manager approval on each machine, and staffed hours only, with no machine-tool use between midnight and 6 AM ([Area 51 rules](https://edgerton.mit.edu/mit-students/student-shops/area-51-shop/rules-use)). Expect the SHED's 5-axis mill and wire EDM to be at least as controlled.

## The SHED lists serious metal tools but names no models

The SHED's entire public equipment statement is two sentences. The prototyping space supports "additive manufacturing through a suite of **FDM / SLS / DLP / PolyJet / Metal 3D printers**." The fabrication space "enables CNC machining and subtractive manufacturing by **4-axis & 5-axis mills, lathes, waterjets, vacuum formers, laser cutters & engraves and wire EDM**" ([Capabilities](https://shed.mit.edu/page/capabilities)). A search of all 24 pages on shed.mit.edu for common machine brands (Haas, Tormach, DMG, OMAX, Sodick, Markforged, Desktop Metal, EOS, Formlabs, Stratasys, Epilog and others) found **zero matches**. The images have empty alt text. make.mit.edu's public equipment database holds **674 records, and none belongs to the SHED**. Its SHED entry has an **empty equipment field** ([make.mit.edu/equipment](https://make.mit.edu/equipment); [make.mit.edu/makerspaces](https://make.mit.edu/makerspaces)). Logging in would not help: the SHED records are already public, and they are empty.

The only brand clue comes from a March 2025 MIT News story. It says the 16.811 turbopump impellers "were printed at the MIT SHED … with support from Tolga Durak … and by industry collaborators at **Desktop Metal**" ([MIT News, 2025-03-28](https://news.mit.edu/2025/preparing-career-forefront-aerospace-industry-0328)). That sentence does **not** establish that the SHED owns a Desktop Metal machine. Desktop Metal could have printed some of the parts itself. It does show metal printing at the SHED being used for course work under staff supervision.

## CNC is open and often free elsewhere; campus metal printing is not

MIT's open makerspaces are being reorganized. A March 5, 2026 letter merges the **Hobby Shop, Project Manus (Metropolis and The Deep) and the Edgerton Center shops into one organization under the Edgerton Center**, targeted for July 1, 2026. The same letter moves The Deep's programming to the basement of Building 6C and starts the Hobby Shop's free-student pilot ([GUE letter](https://orgchart.mit.edu/letters/strengthening-mits-makerspace-ecosystem)). One result is that **The Deep (37-072) is now "AeroAstro The Deep"**, open only to people "affiliated with the department via curriculum, employment, or sponsored teams" ([The Deep iCal feed](https://project-manus.libcal.com/ical_subscribe.php?src=p&cid=9476)). Some make.mit.edu and design.mit.edu pages still describe it as open to everyone. Those pages are out of date.

For a student with no special affiliation, the **Hobby Shop is the strongest free option this term**. It has a CNC mill, a CNC lathe, a waterjet and welding under one roof, the longest posted hours (Mon and Fri 10–5, Tue and Thu 10–8, Wed 10–7, Sat 10–2) and "one-on-one training for every tool you'll need" ([Hobby Shop](https://studentlife.mit.edu/campus-communities/hobby-shop/)). **Edgerton 6C** gives you structured CNC fundamentals. Its training is four 3-hour evening sessions, ending with a steel flashlight you make yourself. Sessions run once or twice a month for six to eight students, so expect a wait. Experienced machinists can fast-track through a safety lecture ([6C training](https://edgerton.mit.edu/for-MIT-students/student-shops/edgerton-6c-student-shop/training)). The only posted 6C hours are from spring 2026, so ask for fall hours ([6C Student Shop](https://edgerton.mit.edu/for-MIT-students/student-shops/edgerton-6c-student-shop)). Note that make.mit.edu lists a Bridgeport EZTrak and a Monarch lathe at 6C, not the Haas lathe, so confirm the Haas lathe is still running. **Five-axis milling and wire EDM**, the SHED's rarest listed tools, also exist at Area 51 (team-only), at the Center for Bits and Atoms (a Hurco VM10U 5-axis mill and a Sodick SL-400G wire EDM) and at the BioInstrumentation Lab (Charmilles wire EDM) ([make.mit.edu/equipment](https://make.mit.edu/equipment)). Student access policies at CBA and BioInstrumentation were not verified.

**Metal 3D printing is a different story.** make.mit.edu answers its own FAQ directly: "**No, there is no 3D printing service on campus**," because the old Project Manus/CopyTech service was discontinued ([make.mit.edu/help](https://make.mit.edu/help)). Its 674-machine database contains **no metal additive machine at all** ([make.mit.edu/equipment](https://make.mit.edu/equipment)). The only verified campus metal printers belong to the **MIT Center for Advanced Production Technologies (APT)**: an **EOS M100** laser powder-bed fusion machine (100 mm diameter build plate, stainless steels), a **Desktop Metal/ExOne InnoventX** binder jetter (sintering is done off-site) and an **Insstek MX-Lab** DED system. Each one sits in a faculty lab, with access "managed directly by the faculty member and their research staff on an ad hoc basis." You request access through APT's intake form ([apt.mit.edu](https://apt.mit.edu/)). MIT.nano's 24/7 prototyping floor has polymer printers only. *Inferred:* for a one-off personal metal part, the dependable route is an outside DMLS vendor from MIT's outsource list, which includes Protolabs, Xometry, Craftcloud, Materialise and Sculpteo ([make.mit.edu/outsource](https://make.mit.edu/outsource)). Protolabs and Xometry are known DMLS providers ([Protolabs DMLS](https://www.protolabs.com/services/3d-printing/direct-metal-laser-sintering/); [Xometry](https://www.xometry.com/capabilities/3d-printing-service/)). Inside MIT, the realistic in-house routes are the SHED, if it says yes, or a UROP, thesis or class in an APT-affiliated lab.

## Conclusion

The SHED's public footprint suggests a lab that serves research collaborators more than walk-in makers. Its site was republished as recently as July 2026, yet it still publishes no hours, trainings or inventory, and its student training track ended in early 2025. *Inferred:* the most promising pitch is a project that gives the SHED something to work with, such as a course tie-in, a research question or a mentoring commitment. "Can I use the 5-axis?" is less likely to succeed. Treat a yes as a bonus, not as the plan.

The bigger shift this year is in the default options. The Edgerton consolidation, the free Hobby Shop and The Deep's move to AeroAstro-only access have made **CNC more accessible to unaffiliated students and the waterjet more concentrated**: the Hobby Shop is now the main walk-up waterjet. Metal additive manufacturing remains the one capability MIT does not offer students as a service. Until the SHED answers, budget for an outside DMLS quote, and spend October getting CNC-trained at the Hobby Shop or 6C. Those skills transfer to any SHED checkout that follows.
