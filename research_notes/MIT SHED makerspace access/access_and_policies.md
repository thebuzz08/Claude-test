## 1. What is the SHED at MIT officially (name, location, operator, opening date), and how does it relate to Project Manus, Metropolis, Mobius, and other makerspaces?

### Takeaway
At MIT, "the SHED" is the **Safety, Health, Environmental Discovery Lab**. It is run by **MIT Environment, Health & Safety (EHS)**, not by Project Manus, MechE, or the Schwarzman College. It is on the **4th floor of Building N52 (N52-496 area), inside the EHS office and lab suite at 265 Massachusetts Ave**. Its founding director is Dr. Tolga Durak, EHS Managing Director. It is separate from Project Manus's Metropolis and The Deep, but it is part of the same campus making network: it lists its events on the shared "MIT Making" LibCal calendar and hosts sessions of the first-year M1T training. I found no opening date.

The site title is "Safety Health Environmental Discovery Lab" — [Project Manus LibCal "EHS The SHED" calendar](https://project-manus.libcal.com/calendar/shed); [shed.mit.edu Community page](https://shed.mit.edu/?page_id=6); [EHS-hosted mirror](https://ehs-wp-spare-1.mit.edu/)
- **Operator:** "The SHED (Safety Health Environmental Discovery lab) is a makerspace managed by MIT EHS, accelerating safe and broad access to advanced technologies." — [MIT Making LibCal, "EHS The SHED" calendar](https://project-manus.libcal.com/calendar/shed). The calendar itself is titled "EHS The SHED."

### Inferences
- The SHED is **not** a School of Engineering, MechE, or Schwarzman College facility. It sits administratively under EHS, which is in central administration and not an academic department. Its focus is research on making safety plus hands-on access. This framing is inferred from the "managed by MIT EHS" language and the research-lab self-description.
- It is **not the same as** Metropolis, The Deep, or Mobius. It does appear connected to Project Manus's systems, given the shared LibCal calendar, hosted M1T sessions, and Culpepper as a co-researcher. It likely uses Mobius for credentials like other MIT shops, but I did not verify this.
- Because the SHED shares a suite with EHS offices, entry probably requires passing through the EHS office area. This may limit unescorted after-hours access, but that is an inference only.

### Gaps
- **Opening date:** Not found. SHED-linked publications date from about 2020 onward, but no launch announcement turned up. Durak's 2021 MIT News interview does not name the SHED in the snippets I saw.
- Whether the SHED is listed as a space in the Mobius app is unverified.
- Some search summaries attributed a full equipment list to the SHED: "4-axis & 5-axis mills, lathes, waterjets, vacuum formers, laser cutters & engravers, wire EDM," plus a "Beamworks" optics/femtosecond-laser training area. ## 2. Who is eligible (undergrads, grads, postdocs, staff)? Fees, membership tiers, or course-affiliation requirements?

### Takeaway
Official SHED pages describe access as "equitable" and open to "the broader MIT community." That suggests MIT students, and probably staff and researchers, are eligible. However, I found **no SHED-specific eligibility rules, fees, or membership tiers**. The only concrete student pathway documented is the **free** first-year M1T program, some of whose sessions take place at the SHED.

### Cited Findings
- The SHED offers "equitable access to advanced technologies for the broader community" — [About the SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/about-the-shed)
- It is described as "empower[ing] members of the MIT community" — [About the SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/about-the-shed)
- **Mentors:** "Student and researcher mentors are the SHED's community leaders, from designing trainings to hosting events and open hours to determining future direction and priorities for the space" — [MIT SHED home / About](https://shed.mit.edu/). This shows both students and researchers take part.
- **M1T eligibility:** M1T is open to all MIT first-year undergraduates, including first-year transfer students. It is free. Completing it gives access to Metropolis and The Deep, a toolbox, a T-shirt, and $50 in Makerbucks for materials — [M1T FAQ](https://make.mit.edu/makerlodge/faq); [M1T page](https://make.mit.edu/m1t)
- **M1T tracks:** Digital Fabrication (FDM 3D printer and laser cutter, one 2-hour session); Manual Fabrication (bandsaw, drill press, sanders, one 2-hour session); Device class such as the Digital Clock (laser cutter, 3D printer, basic soldering, two 2-hour sessions) — [M1T FAQ](https://make.mit.edu/makerlodge/faq). SHED-hosted M1T device projects included a Zoetrope and a Lixie Clock — [LibCal](https://project-manus.libcal.com/event/14128285)
- **Comparison point:** Project Manus's Metropolis and The Deep are "open to all members of the MIT community (students, staff, and faculty)" — [Project Manus Our System](https://project-manus.mit.edu/thedeep-metropolis.html)

### Inferences
- Upper-level undergrads, grad students, and postdocs are probably eligible, based on the "broader MIT community" and "researcher mentors" language. No SHED page I could see stated this explicitly.
- No evidence of a membership fee was found. Do not assume one either way.

### Gaps
- No SHED-specific eligibility statement, fee schedule, membership tier, or course-affiliation requirement was found. Full SHED pages could not be fetched.

## 3. Step-by-step onboarding (safety training, Atlas/EHS courses, Mobius, card access, machine checkouts)

### Takeaway
I found **no SHED-specific onboarding procedure**. The documented MIT-wide pattern, which the SHED most likely follows as an EHS-run space, has five steps:
1. Complete general online shop safety training in **Atlas**.
2. Attend an in-person orientation or training session, booked through the **MIT Making LibCal calendar**.
3. Have trained tool credentials recorded in **Mobius**.
4. Get space access granted by the facility manager.
5. Complete machine-specific checkouts with mentors or staff.

First-years can enter through **M1T** sessions held at the SHED.

### Cited Findings
- **General MIT pattern:** "You need general safety training and training on specific tools before you get to work" — [Project Manus Access Resources](https://project-manus.mit.edu/making-at-mit/access-resources.html); [Getting Trained](https://project-manus.mit.edu/making-at-mit/getting-trained.html)
- **Atlas courses:** Online safety training is two Atlas courses, "Intro to MIT Shop Safety Rules" and "Hazard Communication." After joining the relevant group, Atlas lists the required courses under "My Training Needs," and "the Facility Manager will provide access after completion of training is verified" — [search summary of Project Manus / EHS access pages](https://project-manus.mit.edu/making-at-mit/access-resources.html)
- **Mobius:** Mobius "identifies the training you need for certain makerspaces and helps you contact the right person." It shows "which spaces you can access and who you need to contact" — [About Mobius](https://web.mit.edu/project-manus/mobius/about-mobius.html)
- **Mobius credentials:** These are "created by shop managers once they have trained a student on how to properly use a piece of equipment," and are visible to the student and to MIT shop managers across shops — [Mobius Credentials](https://project-manus.mit.edu/mobius/mobius-credentials.html)
- **Mobius history:** Mobius launched in 2016. It lets users find when spaces are open, learn whom to contact, obtain training, and pay for materials and machine time — [search summary of Project Manus / MIT Better World](https://betterworld.mit.edu/spectrum/issues/spring-2022/passport-for-mit-makerspaces-helps-community-put-creativity-into-practice)
- **EHS framework:** EHS's "Shop and Makerspace Safety" guidance "establish[es] a framework for classifying and safely using tools and equipment and outlin[es] training and supervision requirements" — [EHS Shops and Makerspaces](https://ehs.mit.edu/workplace-safety-program/shops-and-makerspaces/)
- **EHS laser cutter guidance:** Also exists — [EHS Laser Cutter Safety](https://ehs.mit.edu/workplace-safety-program/laser-cutter-safety/); [Laser Cutter Safety Guidance, Jan 8, 2024 (PDF)](https://ehs.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/MITEHS_Laser_Cutter_Guidance.pdf); [Laser Cutter Best Practices Checklist (PDF)](https://ehs.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/MITEHS_Laser_Cutter_Best_Practices_Checklist_for_Safe_Operation.pdf)
- **SHED trainings:** SHED mentors design the trainings and host events and open hours — [MIT SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/)
- **Scheduling:** SHED events and trainings are listed on the MIT Making LibCal "EHS The SHED" calendar — [LibCal SHED calendar](https://project-manus.libcal.com/calendar/shed)
- **Project Manus comparison:** Metropolis and The Deep require a 30-minute orientation, which grants "instant access to both makerspaces" — [Project Manus Students](https://project-manus.mit.edu/students/index.html)

### Inferences
- The most likely SHED path for a student in 2026, inferred and **not verified**:
  1. Complete the EHS/Atlas shop safety courses.
  2. Sign up for a SHED training or open-hours session on the LibCal SHED calendar. First-years can instead take an M1T session held at the SHED.
  3. Get a tool checkout from a SHED mentor or staff member, recorded as a Mobius credential.
  4. Use the space during mentor-hosted open hours.
- Because the SHED is run by EHS itself, its training and supervision rules likely follow, at minimum, the EHS Shop and Makerspace Safety framework.

### Gaps
- No SHED-specific steps, required Atlas course numbers, card-access process, or machine certification list were found.
- I could not verify whether SHED access is tracked in Mobius.

## 4. Hours, staffed vs. unstaffed use, 24/7 access, reservations, working alone or buddy rules

### Takeaway
The only documented SHED access model is **mentor-hosted "open hours" and scheduled training events**, posted on LibCal. I found **no evidence of 24/7 or unstaffed card access** to the SHED, and no SHED-specific rules on working alone or buddy requirements.

### Cited Findings
- SHED mentors host "events and open hours" — [MIT SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/)
- The schedule of hours for MIT makerspaces is kept on the MIT Making LibCal, which has a dedicated "EHS The SHED" calendar — [LibCal SHED calendar](https://project-manus.libcal.com/calendar/shed); [MIT Making LibCal](https://project-manus.libcal.com/)
- **Comparison:** Some other MIT makerspaces offer 24/7 tap-card access, typically requested by email to that space — [search summary, Project Manus access pages](https://project-manus.mit.edu/making-at-mit/access-resources.html)
- **Comparison:** Project Manus mentors "get off-hours access to the space as well as a materials allowance" — [Become a Mentor](https://make.mit.edu/mentor)

### Inferences
- Since the SHED is inside the EHS office and lab suite, use is probably limited to staffed or mentor-hosted times. This is unverified.

### Gaps
- Actual weekly hours, any reservation or booking system for SHED machines beyond LibCal events, card-access policy, and working-alone/buddy rules were not found.

## 5. Material costs, consumables, printing fees, personal vs. coursework projects

### Takeaway
I found no SHED-specific material, consumable, or printing fee policy. MIT-wide, materials and machine time at Project Manus spaces are paid through Mobius. M1T graduates get $50 in Makerbucks.

### Cited Findings
- The Mobius web app "permits students to pay for materials, machine time, access fees, and more" — [search summary of Project Manus / Mobius pages](https://web.mit.edu/project-manus/mobius/about-mobius.html)
- M1T completers receive $50 in Makerbucks to spend on materials — [M1T FAQ](https://make.mit.edu/makerlodge/faq)

### Inferences
- If the SHED charges for materials, it would most likely do so through Mobius. This is unverified.

### Gaps
- No SHED fee schedule and no personal vs. coursework project policy were found.
