## Q1. Which MIT spaces have CNC mills/lathes/waterjets usable by students, and what are the access requirements (fees, training, eligibility, hours)?

### Takeaway
Several MIT spaces give students CNC or manual machining. The most open, low-cost route for any student is the **Edgerton 6C Student Shop** (manual and CNC mills and lathes, open to any student after a 12-hour training) and **The Deep** (MAD Making / ex-Project Manus, Bldg 37: milling, turning, waterjet, open to all MIT community). The **Hobby Shop** (N51, paid membership) and **MITERS** (student-run, CNC mill) are other options. **Pappalardo** (MechE), **Area 51** (Edgerton clubs and teams), **MakerWorkshop** (MechE and Trust Center) and the **LMP shop** limit who can use them.

### Cited Findings

**MAD Making (formerly Project Manus): The Deep and Metropolis**
- Project Manus has been renamed "MAD Making" and moved under the MIT Morningside Academy for Design (MAD). It runs two staffed makerspaces: The Deep (Building 37) and Metropolis (Building 6C) — [MAD Making, design.mit.edu](https://design.mit.edu/about/mad-making); [Making, design.mit.edu](https://design.mit.edu/about/making)
- The Deep (37-072) offers milling, turning, SLA 3D printing, mold making and **waterjet**. Metropolis (6C-006B) offers welding, laser cutting, FDM 3D printing, basic electronics, sewing, woodworking tools and small screen printing. Metropolis has **no CNC mill and no metal printer** — [Project Manus "Our System"](https://project-manus.mit.edu/thedeep-metropolis.html); [MAD Labs & Facilities](https://mad.mit.edu/making/labs-and-facilities)
- Both are "open to all members of the MIT community (students, staff, and faculty)". Metropolis is run by student mentors together with staff — [Project Manus Students page](https://project-manus.mit.edu/students/index.html); [MIT News, The Deep opening, 2019](https://news.mit.edu/2019/mit-welcome-to-the-deep-new-project-manus-makerspace-0408)
- The Deep began training students in summer 2018 and opened formally in 2019 — [MIT News 2019](https://news.mit.edu/2019/mit-welcome-to-the-deep-new-project-manus-makerspace-0408)
- The Deep's events and open hours are posted on LibCal under the name "MAD The Deep" — [project-manus.libcal.com/calendar/thedeep](https://project-manus.libcal.com/calendar/thedeep)

**Edgerton Center 6C Student Shop (6C-006A)**
- The shop moved from Building 44 to 6C-006A and reopened in 2020. It has hand-operated mills and lathes, **CNC lathes and a CNC machining center**, 3D printers and precision tools — [MIT News, Feb 2020](https://news.mit.edu/2020/edgerton-center-celebrates-reopening-student-shop-0204)
- Training is monthly and held in the evenings: **12 hours of hands-on instruction**. Students learn milling and lathe basics by making parts, ending with a steel flashlight. **After the training, MIT students from across campus can use the shop for research and personal projects** — [MIT News 2020](https://news.mit.edu/2020/edgerton-center-celebrates-reopening-student-shop-0204); [Edgerton 6C training page](https://edgerton.mit.edu/for-MIT-students/student-shops/edgerton-6c-student-shop/training)

**Edgerton "Area 51" CNC Shop (N51, 1st floor)**
- The shop has CNC milling and lathe machines, an injection molding machine, a thermoforming machine and a **waterjet** — [Edgerton Area 51 shop](https://edgerton.mit.edu/for-MIT-students/student-shops/area-51-shop)
- **Eligibility is restricted** to students on Edgerton clubs and teams, D-Lab, and the International Design Center community. Training is for registered students active on an Edgerton club or team, who learn by making parts for their team. To sign up, email the shop manager (snippet names Pat McAtamney, patmca@mit.edu; this may be out of date) — [Area 51 training](https://edgerton.mit.edu/mit-students/student-shops/area-51-shop/training)

**MIT Hobby Shop (moved to N51 in fall 2023)**
- A fully equipped wood and metal shop with three professional staff. Any MIT affiliate can buy a membership. It has more than 200 members per semester. It moved from W31-031 to renovated space in N51 (the former MIT Museum store) in fall 2023 — [MIT Alumni "Hobby Shop Rebuilt"](https://alum.mit.edu/slice/mit-hobby-shop-rebuilt); [Tech Review 2024](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/02/28/1087648/mit-hobby-shop-rebuilt/)
- CNC equipment includes a **Techno CNC 4x8 router**, which runs daily on member projects — [Techno CNC case study](https://www.technocnc.com/mit-hobby-shop/). An older IAP class taught programming and running a **Harrison Alpha 300 CNC lathe** ($80 for materials plus membership) — [IAP 2019 Hobby Shop listings](http://web.mit.edu/iap/www/iap19/iap/ns157.html)
- **Fees:** alumni pay $200/term or $500/year. Retirees pay $60/term or $150/year. Members must attend a new-member orientation — [Alumni snippet](https://alum.mit.edu/slice/mit-hobby-shop-rebuilt); [studentlife.mit.edu Hobby Shop](https://studentlife.mit.edu/campus-communities/hobby-shop). I found **no current student fee**. An old blog post (circa 2012) suggests about $30/term or $75/year for students, which is **likely outdated** — [orangenarwhals 2012](https://orangenarwhals.com/2012/03/how-do-i-machine-shop-at-mit/)

**MechE Pappalardo Lab (Building 3)**
- This is the Course 2 undergraduate teaching lab and "MIT's primary machine shop". It has multiple lathes, CNC mills, a large waterjet and 3D printers — [The Tech photo caption](https://thetech.com/photos/3755); [MIT Admissions blog "Machine Shops Part 2"](https://mitadmissions.org/blogs/entry/machine_shops_part_2/)
- Access comes **through classes** (2.009 in fall, 2.670 in IAP, 2.007 in spring, plus summer programs). It is "not for personal projects", and access is generally limited to Course 2 students and class work — [MIT Admissions blog](https://mitadmissions.org/blogs/entry/machine_shops_part_2/); [adviseme.mit.edu 2013](http://adviseme.mit.edu/2013/06/machine-shop-access-around-mit/). These sources are old, so check current policy.

**MIT MakerWorkshop (MechE / Martin Trust Center)**
- Open to students, faculty and staff affiliated with MechE and the Trust Center. Members join by attending "Maker Monday". Equipment includes a mill, lathe, **waterjet**, laser cutters and 3D printers. Each machine needs its own training. Student mentors say they cannot give formal mill or lathe training — [makerworks.mit.edu Access](http://makerworks.mit.edu/access/); [Machine Specific Training](http://makerworks.mit.edu/access/machine-specific-training/). The site shows a "WE'RE MOVING TO" banner — [makerworks.mit.edu](http://makerworks.mit.edu/) — so the space may have moved. Status unverified.

**MITERS (MIT Electronics Research Society)**
- MIT's student-run, member-run shop, open to all MIT affiliates. It has two manual mills, a **CNC mill**, a lathe, band saws and 3D printers — [oldmiters.mit.edu](http://oldmiters.mit.edu/); [MakerWorkshop/MITERS search snippet](http://adviseme.mit.edu/2013/06/machine-shop-access-around-mit/)

**LMP (Lab for Manufacturing and Productivity) Shop**
- The shop welcomes researchers. Most users get access through classes. **Using the waterjet and 3D printers requires a cost object** (an MIT research or department account), so it is not set up for personal student projects — [lmp-shop.mit.edu](https://lmp-shop.mit.edu/)

### Inferences
- For a student with no team or class affiliation who wants **CNC milling or lathe work**: the Edgerton 6C Student Shop (free; 12-hour training) or the Hobby Shop (membership fee) are the most direct routes. **The Deep** is the main open-access **waterjet**.
- For a student on an Edgerton team (e.g., Solar Car, Rocket Team, Motorsports), Area 51 adds CNC and waterjet capacity.
- I found no fee for the Edgerton 6C shop or The Deep, so they may be free to students. This is inferred; I could not confirm it.

- Whether Metropolis or The Deep changed after the MAD transition or a move into the Metropolitan Warehouse building (the Met was being renovated 2022-2025 to house MAD and "makerspaces such as Project Manus") — [MAD/Met snippet](https://news.mit.edu/2022/morningside-academy-design-0314).

## Q2. Which MIT spaces (if any) have metal 3D printers, and can students use them or submit jobs?

### Takeaway
I found **no student-facing, open-access metal 3D printer or metal-printing job service** at MIT. Metal additive equipment does exist in research facilities, notably **APT (Additive Processing and Technologies)**, part of LMP and led by Prof. John Hart (a Desktop Metal co-founder). APT's equipment includes an EOS M100 laser powder-bed fusion (DMLS/SLM) machine and a Desktop Metal/ExOne InnoventX binder jetter. Access looks to be for research use or by cost object, not for walk-in students. MIT's only campus print-job service (Project Manus + CopyTech) printed **PLA only** and **closed December 2023**.

### Cited Findings
- APT (apt.mit.edu) runs "state-of-the-art advanced manufacturing facilities on campus, with a core focus on additive manufacturing". Equipment includes a **Desktop Metal/ExOne InnoventX binder jetting system** (metals and ceramics) and an **EOS M100 laser powder-bed fusion system ("DMLS"/"SLM")** — [APT](https://apt.mit.edu/) (snippet only)
- LMP's director is John Hart. Using LMP shop 3D printers and the waterjet requires a cost object — [lmp-shop.mit.edu](https://lmp-shop.mit.edu/); [MIT LMP directory](https://officesdirectory.mit.edu/lmp)
- Desktop Metal was founded by an MIT alumnus and four MIT professors. Its Studio System is an office-friendly metal printing system — [MIT News 2018](https://news.mit.edu/2018/desktop-metal-3-d-printing-production-studio-1004). This does **not** establish that a Studio System is available to students on campus.
- The **MIT 3D Printing Service** (Project Manus + MIT CopyTech, 11-004, submitted through 3DPrinterOS) printed **PLA only** (MIT red, white, light blue, gray). It **reached end of life in December 2023**. MIT now points users to make.mit.edu/outsource — [Project Manus 3D Printing Service](https://project-manus.mit.edu/3d-printing-service); [3D Printing FAQ](https://project-manus.mit.edu/3d-printing-service/3d-printing-faq)
- Listed makerspace 3D printing is plastic only: The Deep (SLA), Metropolis (FDM), N52 fabrication shop (FDM, SLS, SLA) — [Project Manus/MAD snippets](https://mad.mit.edu/making/labs-and-facilities); [design.mit.edu Making](https://design.mit.edu/about/making)
- A search restricted to mit.edu for "Metal X" / "Desktop Metal Studio" turned up **no MIT makerspace or shop listing a Markforged Metal X or Desktop Metal Studio System** — [search across mit.edu results, e.g. Edgerton Student Shops and Makerspaces](https://edgerton.mit.edu/for-MIT-students/student-shops-and-makerspaces)
- MIT Professional Education runs paid additive manufacturing courses ("Additive Manufacturing: From Prototyping to Production"). These are for professionals, not a print service — [MIT Professional Education AM course](http://professional-education.mit.edu/am3d)

### Inferences
- A student who needs metal AM on campus would most likely need (a) a research lab or UROP connection to APT or LMP, with a PI's cost object, or (b) a class tied to APT or Hart's group (e.g., MechE additive manufacturing courses). This is inferred from the cost-object rule and APT's research focus.
- Another option is fused-filament metal (bound-metal) printing. No MIT space was confirmed to have the debind/sinter setup this needs. Asking in MITERS, the Hobby Shop, or student team shops would be speculative.

### Gaps
- APT's access policy, recharge rates and whether students can submit jobs. The page was blocked.
- Whether the Center for Bits and Atoms (cba.mit.edu), MIT.nano / Deshpande, Beaver Works, Sloan or DMSE labs have student-usable metal AM. I did not have the tool budget to search these individually, and their pages are blocked. MIT.nano is a nano/microfabrication facility and probably irrelevant to metal printing at part scale (inferred).
- Student-team metal printers (e.g., Rocket Team, Motorsports) are possible but not found.

## Q3. How do Mobius / Project Manus / MAKE tie these together?

### Takeaway
Project Manus (started 2015) built MIT's "makersystem": the **Mobius** web app for finding spaces and tracking training, and later the **MAKE** hub at make.mit.edu, which lists 35-40+ makerspaces, training credentials and equipment. Project Manus is now **"MAD Making"** under the Morningside Academy for Design. make.mit.edu appears to be the current front door. Each space still runs its own training and access rules.

### Cited Findings
- Project Manus was started in October 2015 by Provost Martin Schmidt to develop "the gold standard in next generation academic makersystems" — [Project Manus snippet](https://project-manus.mit.edu/the-latest.html)
- **Mobius** "connects students, faculty and researchers with nearby resources". It helps users find materials, machines and spaces, see which spaces they can access, and identify the training needed for each makerspace — [Project Manus, Paying for Things / Mobius](https://project-manus.mit.edu/mobius/paying-for-things.html); [Getting Trained](https://project-manus.mit.edu/making-at-mit/getting-trained.html)
- **MAKE (make.mit.edu)** is "the central hub for makerspaces, shops, maker communities and making on the MIT campus". It keeps a directory of makerspaces, **maker training credentials** and equipment lists, covering 35+ (elsewhere "over 40") makerspaces — [MIT MAKE](https://make.mit.edu/); [MIT Makerspaces](https://make.mit.edu/makerspaces)
- Project Manus has become "MAD Making" within the Morningside Academy for Design — [design.mit.edu MAD Making](https://design.mit.edu/about/mad-making)
- **M1T (Makerspace First-Year Training)**, formerly MakerLodge, is open to all first-year undergraduates. It covers FDM/laser (digital fab), bandsaw/drill press/sanders (manual fab), or a device class, and gives access to key makerspaces plus "Makerbucks" for supplies — [make.mit.edu/m1t](https://make.mit.edu/m1t); [design.mit.edu MakerLodge becomes M1T](https://design.mit.edu/news/makerlodge-becomes-m1t)
- Community makerspaces listed as open to students include: the Edgerton Student Project Lab, Edgerton Student Shop, Electronics Mezzanine Lab, Hobby Shop, Metropolis, MIT MakerWorkshop and MITERS — [engineering.mit.edu/makerspaces](https://engineering.mit.edu/makerspaces)

### Inferences
- Mobius looks to have been largely replaced by the MAKE site (make.mit.edu) as the directory and credential system. This is inferred: recent pages refer to make.mit.edu while Mobius appears mostly on older Project Manus pages. I could not confirm whether the Mobius app still runs in 2026.
- M1T does **not** include CNC or metal machining. CNC needs separate, space-specific training (e.g., the Edgerton 12-hour course).

### Gaps
- Whether Mobius still exists, and whether credentials earned in one space (e.g., the Edgerton mill/lathe course) carry over to others (e.g., The Deep's mills).
- Current Makerbucks / "paying for things" mechanics. The page was blocked.

## Q4. Costs and typical training durations; external services for metal prints

### Takeaway
Most student-facing shops appear free or low-cost but require training. The Edgerton 6C mill/lathe training is **12 hours** (evenings, monthly). The Hobby Shop charges a membership fee: the student rate is unconfirmed, while alumni pay $200/term or $500/year. Research facilities (LMP, APT) charge a cost object. For metal 3D printing, students realistically use **commercial services**. MIT's own outsource list includes Protolabs, Xometry, Shapeways, Materialise, Sculpteo, Craftcloud and others.

### Cited Findings
- Edgerton 6C: 12 hours of hands-on evening training, held monthly — [MIT News 2020](https://news.mit.edu/2020/edgerton-center-celebrates-reopening-student-shop-0204)
- Hobby Shop: alumni $200/term or $500/year. Retirees $60/term or $150/year. Members must attend an orientation. Student fee not found — [alum.mit.edu](https://alum.mit.edu/slice/mit-hobby-shop-rebuilt); [studentlife.mit.edu](https://studentlife.mit.edu/campus-communities/hobby-shop)
- Hobby Shop CNC lathe IAP class: $80 for materials and membership (2019) — [IAP 2019](http://web.mit.edu/iap/www/iap19/iap/ns157.html)
- LMP waterjet and 3D printers: cost object required — [lmp-shop.mit.edu](https://lmp-shop.mit.edu/)
- MIT's outsource list (make.mit.edu/outsource), created after the 3D Printing Service closed in December 2023, includes Shapeways, 3D Hubs, 3D Systems, Materialise, Staples, Sculpteo, **Protolabs**, Stratasys, Craftcloud and **Xometry** — [Project Manus 3D Printing Service page / make.mit.edu/outsource](https://project-manus.mit.edu/making-at-mit/3d-printing-service.html)
- Xometry offers DMLS (metal) 3D printing quotes alongside SLS, FDM, SLA, Carbon DLS, PolyJet and MJF — [Xometry 3D printing](https://www.xometry.com/capabilities/3d-printing-service/)
- Protolabs offers DMLS metal 3D printing — [Protolabs DMLS](https://www.protolabs.com/services/3d-printing/direct-metal-laser-sintering/)

### Inferences
- For a one-off metal printed part, an external DMLS service (Xometry, Protolabs, Craftcloud/Hubs) is the most realistic route for an individual MIT student. Student teams can sometimes pay with team or Edgerton funds (inferred).
- SendCutSend (laser- or waterjet-cut sheet metal, not 3D printing) does not appear on MIT's outsource list in the snippets I saw. It is commonly used by hobbyists for flat metal parts, but I found no MIT-specific source.

### Gaps
- Training time for The Deep's mill, lathe and waterjet, and for the Hobby Shop orientation.
- Any 2025-2026 fee changes. All fee data comes from undated or older snippets.
- I found no Reddit r/mit or The Tech discussion of metal 3D printing access. Reddit is not reachable with these tools.
