Research date: 2026-10-01. **Important method caveat:** every `*.mit.edu` host (shed.mit.edu, make.mit.edu, news.mit.edu, project-manus.mit.edu) and project-manus.libcal.com was **blocked by the network egress proxy** in this research environment (HTTP 403 on CONNECT, organization policy). web.archive.org was also unavailable. So I could not open any page in full. All findings below come from **search-engine snippets** of official MIT pages. They are accurate to the snippets, but I could not read the full page context, any equipment tables, or page dates. Treat model-level detail as **not verified**.

## Q0: What and where is "the SHED"? (identity check)

### Takeaway
"The SHED" at MIT is the **Safety, Health, and Environmental Discovery lab (SHED)**. MIT Environment, Health & Safety (EHS) manages it, Dr. Tolga Durak is its founding director, and it sits on the **4th floor of Building N52** (265 Massachusetts Ave / Front St entrance). It is a different place from RIT's much better known "SHED" (Student Hall for Exploration and Development), which dominates search results.

### Cited Findings
- The SHED (Safety Health Environmental Discovery lab) "is a makerspace managed by MIT EHS, accelerating safe and broad access to advanced technologies" — [Project Manus LibCal "EHS The SHED" calendar](https://project-manus.libcal.com/calendar/shed) (search snippet)
- Location (from the snippet text of the M1T event listings): "Building N52, 4th Floor... enter N52 from the 265 Massachusetts Ave / Front St entrance, take the elevator or stairs to the 4th floor and enter the EHS office and lab area, then proceed straight from the EHS entrance to the SHED" — [LibCal: Zoetrope Project at the SHED](https://project-manus.libcal.com/event/13637298); [LibCal: Lixie Clock Project at the SHED](https://project-manus.libcal.com/event/14128285)
- The SHED's official site gives the address only as "77 Massachusetts Avenue, Cambridge, MA" (MIT's generic address), contact SHED@mit.edu — [MIT SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/)
- It is "MIT's first multidisciplinary laboratory dedicated to prototyping, fabrication, and scientific discovery". Tolga Durak is Founding Director and PI, and he also leads the MIT EHS Management System — [Tolga Durak, MIT NSE](https://nse.mit.edu/people/tolga-durak/); [About the SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/about-the-shed)
- The SHED "proactively integrates research, applied problem-solving and core Environment, Health and Safety expertise to develop knowledge and processes that accelerate safe and broad access to advanced technologies" — [MIT SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/)
- Confusion warning: RIT's SHED (Rochester Institute of Technology, opened fall 2023) has detailed public equipment documentation (Bernina textiles, Roland GS24, Keithley/Tektronix electronics, an "Atrium Makerspace" with industrial 3D printing, wood and metal shops, CNC and welding). **None of that is MIT** — [RIT SHED Makerspaces](https://www.rit.edu/shed/makerspaces); [RIT SHED docs](https://docs.make.rit.edu/)
- A separate shop in the same building, the "MAD N52 Shop" (Morningside Academy for Design), also appears on make.mit.edu. It should not be merged with the SHED — [MIT MAKE](https://make.mit.edu/)

### Inferences
- The SHED is not a Project Manus/MAD-run student makerspace like Metropolis. It is an **EHS-run research and prototyping lab** that also offers student-facing programming, such as the M1T first-year training alternative. That likely shapes access: access is probably more research- and mentor-mediated than walk-in.
- N52 at 265 Mass Ave is the building that houses MIT EHS offices, which fits with EHS running the space.

### Gaps
- Opening date, square footage, and hours could not be found. The full About page was not fetchable.

## Q1: Full machine list with models

### Takeaway
The official SHED "Capabilities"/"Spaces" page lists equipment **by category only**: FDM / SLS / DLP / PolyJet / **Metal** 3D printers; 4-axis and 5-axis CNC mills; lathes; waterjets; vacuum formers; laser cutters/engravers; wire EDM; electronics; wet chemistry; and biofabrication. **I found no manufacturer or model names for any SHED machine in any accessible source.**

### Cited Findings
**Prototyping space** (official wording): "includes a variety of hand tools, section for electronics work, supports digital design & modeling and enables additive manufacturing through a suite of FDM/ SLS / DLP / PolyJet / Metal 3D printers" — [SHED Capabilities](https://shed.mit.edu/page/capabilities); [SHED Spaces](https://shed.mit.edu/spaces)

**Fabrication space** (official wording): "enables CNC machining and subtractive manufacturing by 4-axis & 5-axis mills, lathes, waterjets, vacuum formers, laser cutters & engraves and wire EDM" — [SHED Capabilities](https://shed.mit.edu/page/capabilities)

**Wet chemistry / analytical:** "wet-chemistry space with chemical fume hoods, specially designed perchloric acid fume hood with wash-down capability, benches fitted with compressed air, natural gas and deionized water". Capabilities include compound synthesis, analytical/characterization, and computational chemistry — [MIT SHED search summary of shed.mit.edu](https://shed.mit.edu/page/capabilities)

**Industrial hygiene lab:** "Hygieneyx", an IH testing lab (airborne contaminants and silica, metals, organics and microbial, asbestos ID) — [shed.mit.edu](https://shed.mit.edu/)

**Biofabrication suite:** "Bioreactors, 3D Bio printers, biosafety cabinets..." — [SHED Capabilities](https://shed.mit.edu/page/capabilities); [Integrated Biofabrication](https://shed.mit.edu/page/integrated-biofabrication)

**Research pages that suggest further instruments** (titles only): Microfluidics Laser Processing, Boiling Heat Transfer, Brain Tissue Imaging, Environmental Fluid Mechanics, Neutron Scintillators, Health Informatics — [shed.mit.edu research pages](https://shed.mit.edu/tile-page/research)

**Equipment confirmed through student training:** the M1T SHED variants use a **laser cutter, 3D printer, electronics, and soldering** (Zoetrope project: 4 hours rather than the standard 2-hour M1T; Lixie/Digital Clock project: 2 sessions) — [LibCal Zoetrope event](https://project-manus.libcal.com/event/13637298); [LibCal Lixie Clock event](https://project-manus.libcal.com/event/14128285); [make.mit.edu M1T](https://make.mit.edu/m1t)

## Q2: Does it have metal additive manufacturing? Who can run it?

### Takeaway
**Yes, per the official SHED capabilities page**, which lists "Metal 3D printers" in the prototyping space's additive suite. The **make/model is unknown**, and so are its technology (bound-metal/sinter like Markforged Metal X or Desktop Metal Studio, versus powder-bed DMLS/LPBF) and its operating policy.

### Cited Findings
- "...enables additive manufacturing through a suite of FDM/ SLS / DLP / PolyJet / Metal 3D printers" — [SHED Capabilities](https://shed.mit.edu/page/capabilities)
- The SHED says "Student and researcher mentors are prepared to support users in the intricacies of every equipment, tool and process" — [MIT SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/) (search snippet)
- The SHED's mission centers on EHS research to "accelerate safe and broad access to advanced technologies" — [About the SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/about-the-shed)

### Inferences
- Many MIT makerspaces use mentor-mediated or staff-operated models for high-hazard equipment. Metal AM involves fine powders, inert gas, and debinding/sintering furnaces, and an EHS-run lab is likely to treat it as staff/mentor-operated or research-project access rather than open self-serve. **This is unverified.**
- Because the lab doubles as an EHS research site, the metal printer may be there partly to study the hazards of metal AM. This is a guess and is not cited.

### Gaps

- Whether undergraduates can run it directly, use job submission, or get access only through research projects: not found.

## Q3: What CNC equipment exists, and what training is required?

### Takeaway
The SHED lists **4-axis and 5-axis CNC mills, lathes, waterjets, and wire EDM**. This is serious metal-capable subtractive equipment, and the 5-axis mill and wire EDM are rarer than what typical MIT student shops offer. **I found no makes/models and no published CNC training levels.**

### Cited Findings
- "CNC machining and subtractive manufacturing by 4-axis & 5-axis mills, lathes, waterjets, vacuum formers, laser cutters & engraves and wire EDM" — [SHED Capabilities](https://shed.mit.edu/page/capabilities)
- MIT-wide makerspace training runs through make.mit.edu (formerly Project Manus, now under the Morningside Academy for Design). Project Manus content has migrated there — [ehs.mit.edu Shops and Makerspaces / Project Manus notice](https://ehs.mit.edu/workplace-safety-program/shops-and-makerspaces/); [MIT MAKE](https://make.mit.edu/)
- The only SHED training I could confirm is first-year M1T (laser cutter, 3D printer, soldering) — [LibCal Zoetrope event](https://project-manus.libcal.com/event/13637298)
- MIT EHS publishes shop guidance (e.g., laser cutter safety guidance, Jan 8, 2024) that likely applies to the SHED — [MIT EHS Laser Cutter Guidance PDF](https://ehs.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/MITEHS_Laser_Cutter_Guidance.pdf)

### Inferences
- Waterjet, 5-axis, and wire EDM are typically staff-run or available only after advanced, machine-specific checkouts at MIT. I'd expect the same here, but this is not verified.
- The "Making the Makers" research page (a longitudinal study of makerspace learning at MIT and UMich) suggests the SHED studies maker training itself, so its training framework may be research-structured — [Making the Makers](https://shed.mit.edu/page/making-the-makers)

### Gaps
- CNC machine brands (Haas/Tormach/DMG Mori/etc.), waterjet brand (Omax/Flow), and EDM brand: not found.
- Training tiers, checkout requirements, and whether students may run CNC unsupervised: not found.

## Q4: Machines restricted to staff-operated or job-submission use?

### Takeaway
**I found no explicit statement** in accessible sources on which SHED machines are staff-only or job-submission. The only access language is that "student and researcher mentors" support users on every piece of equipment.

### Cited Findings
- "Student and researcher mentors are prepared to support users in the intricacies of every equipment, tool and process" — [MIT SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/)
- The SHED offers "access to MIT faculty, staff, researchers, students, and thought leaders", and it says a "broad mix of people and experience is the most effective way to rapidly develop better risk management, safer use, and more equitable access" — [MIT SHED](https://shed.mit.edu/)
- First-year students can get hands-on use of the laser cutter, 3D printer, and soldering through the SHED M1T variants — [LibCal Zoetrope event](https://project-manus.libcal.com/event/13637298)

### Inferences
- At minimum, laser cutters, (FDM) 3D printers, and soldering stations are student-operable after training, as shown by M1T.
- Higher-hazard tools (metal AM, waterjet, wire EDM, 5-axis) are likely mentor- or staff-mediated, but this is not verified.

### Gaps
- No published access tiers, booking system details, or staff-only lists were reachable. Recommend checking [make.mit.edu/equipment](https://make.mit.edu/equipment), the [SHED LibCal calendar](https://project-manus.libcal.com/calendar/shed), or emailing SHED@mit.edu.
