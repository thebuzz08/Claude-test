# Competitive Landscape: AI "Dark Factory" Automation Planner (ingest business info -> full automation plan with specs)

Research date: 2026-10-04. Dates of each finding are noted inline where known. Search-engine summaries were used for some facts; where a fact came only from an aggregator (CB Insights, Tracxn, Sacra, etc.) or looked unreliable, it is flagged.

## 1. Do startups or products (2024-2026) use AI to generate automation plans, robotic cell designs, factory layouts, or feasibility/ROI assessments from customer inputs?

### Takeaway
Yes, but each one covers only part of the job. The closest analogs are **Robotiq IQ** (June 2026: voice notes, files and 3D scans in, a validated palletizing workcell design out, used through integrator partners), **Vention** (generative-AI design assistant in MachineBuilder plus "Zero-Shot Automation"; it designs cells from Vention's own parts catalog) and **Accenture's Physical AI Orchestrator / Siemens Digital Twin Composer / NVIDIA Omniverse Mega** (enterprise digital-twin layout and simulation). I found no product that takes heterogeneous business inputs (process descriptions, photos/video, floor plans, ERP data, product specs) and outputs a whole-facility automation roadmap with vendor-neutral equipment selection, layout, integration architecture and supply chain. Every close player is single-cell, tied to one vendor's hardware, aimed at large enterprises, or an engineering tool that assumes an expert user.

### Cited Findings

**Closest direct analogs (AI generates cell/line designs from customer inputs)**
- **Robotiq IQ** (launched June 3, 2026): an AI platform that "captures unstructured automation project data, coordinates engineering workflows, and automatically generates validated workcell designs based on real customer inputs and historical deployment data from thousands of previous factory installations." Inputs are voice notes, legacy file uploads and 3D scans of the site. Outputs are validated workcell designs, a 3D digital twin and performance simulation checked against standard engineering rules. Live demos went from application input to a running workcell in about 24 hours. — [Robotics & Automation News, 2026-06-03](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2026/06/03/robotiq-launches-ai-platform-to-automate-robotic-workcell-integration/102227/)
  - Limits: it launched for **robotic palletizing only**, with other applications planned "over time". Integrators and partners are the primary operators, and CEO Samuel Bouchard says "IQ does not replace partner expertise. It amplifies this expertise." Pricing is not disclosed. — [same source](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2026/06/03/robotiq-launches-ai-platform-to-automate-robotic-workcell-integration/102227/)
- **Vention** (Montreal): MachineBuilder is a browser-based automation design tool with real-time pricing and component-compatibility checks. Its generative-AI **Design Assistant** "automatically fills connection points with connector parts, builds subassemblies, and designs enclosures," and dragging a conveyor into a scene auto-adds legs, motors, sensors and plates. Demo Day was Oct 29, 2025. — [Modern Machine Shop / Production Machining, "Can AI automate automation?"](https://www.mmsonline.com/articles/can-ai-automate-automation-); [MachineDesign, Demo Day 2025](https://www.machinedesign.com/automation-iiot/news/55327679/vention-inc-vention-unveils-ai-powered-industrial-automation-breakthroughs-at-demo-day-2025)
  - Vention also showed an "AI Operator" bin-picking product (MachineMotion AI + NVIDIA Isaac + wrist camera) where "all that's needed to program the system is a CAD file of the part," along with a physics Simulation Checker and RemoteView/RemoteSupport. — [MMS](https://www.mmsonline.com/articles/can-ai-automate-automation-)
  - "Zero-Shot Automation" (Demo Day 2025) is pitched as cutting the "integration tax" by letting machines be designed and deployed "without months of custom coding." — [Pulse 2.0 / Dealroom summary](https://pulse2.com/vention-110-million-funding/amp/)
  - Funding: a **$110M USD Series D announced Jan 27, 2026**, led by Investissement Québec with NVentures (NVIDIA), Desjardins and Fidelity Canada. Total raised is above $260M USD. Annual run-rate revenue passed CA$100M in late Dec 2025. Vention is deployed in **4,000+ factories** (Boeing, L'Oréal, Lockheed Martin; elsewhere 3M, Hershey, Pratt & Whitney are cited). Customers are 70% US, 20% Europe and 10% Canada. — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/montreal-industrial-ai-scaleup-vention-raises-110-million-usd-series-d/); [The Logic](https://thelogic.co/news/vention-series-d-industrial-robots-quebec/); [Startbase](https://www.startbase.de/news/vention-sammelt-110-millionen-us-dollar-fuer-den-ausbau-von-physical-ai-in-der-fertigung-ein/). Valuation is reported as ">$1B" in one summary, but BetaKit says it was not disclosed. Treat it as unconfirmed.
  - Gap vs concept: the design is limited to Vention's own modular hardware catalog. The user (or Vention application engineers) must already know which cell to build. It does not ingest ERP data, video or floor plans to find *which* processes to automate, and it does not plan a whole factory.
- **Bright Machines** (San Francisco): "software-defined manufacturing." Its Microfactory is a software-defined production line for high-precision electronics assembly. "Bright Designer" enables "AI-assisted design-to-manufacturing workflows." — [Sacra / CB Insights summaries](https://sacra.com/c/bright-machines)
  - Raised a **$126M Series C in June 2024** ($106M equity led by BlackRock funds, with NVIDIA, Microsoft, Eclipse, Jabil and Shinhan; plus $20M J.P. Morgan venture debt). ### Inferences
- The market is converging on "AI-generated workcell design" from both ends. Component and robot vendors (Robotiq, Vention, ABB) are adding AI to their own design tools, and enterprise twin platforms (Siemens, NVIDIA, Dassault, Accenture) are adding agents to simulation. Robotiq IQ (inputs: conversation, files, 3D scan; output: validated cell design in about 24h) is the strongest evidence the core thesis is technically viable and commercially wanted. It is also the most likely fast follower to expand scope.
- Every close player is **hardware-captive**: Vention designs only with Vention parts, Robotiq with Robotiq grippers and palletizers, Bright Machines with its own Microfactory cells, and Formic with its own fleet. A vendor-neutral "AI automation architect" that picks equipment across vendors (and earns via referral, financing or integration fees) is structurally different and could be a channel partner to all of them.
- The enterprise digital-twin stack (Siemens/NVIDIA/Dassault/Accenture) needs expert operators, CAD/BIM-grade inputs and large budgets, so it is not reachable for US SMBs. The SMB tier is served today mainly by RaaS companies (Formic, RobCo) and integrator pre-sales.
- The heavily funded "AI-native factory" companies (Hadrian, Machina, Mind Robotics, YC factory plays) are betting that it is easier to *own* dark factories than to retrofit other people's. That is a competing thesis and a signal that retrofitting existing SMB plants is seen as hard.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm pricing for Vention MachineBuilder AI, Robotiq IQ, Siemens Digital Twin Composer or Accenture Physical AI Orchestrator.
- I did not find 2025-2026 funding or product news for Realtime Robotics, Arch Systems, Instrumental, Invisible AI or Retrocausal in this pass. Invisible AI and Retrocausal remain vision-based assembly-line analytics firms per [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/invisible-ai-vs-retrocausal). Drishti was acquired by Apple (Sept 2023) per [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/drishti-technologies).
- I could not verify any Autodesk "AI factory design" product release for 2025-26, or any Visual Components or Siemens Tecnomatix Plant Simulation generative-layout feature.
- I found no evidence of an "AI automation scoping" startup that ingests photos, video and ERP data to produce a vendor-neutral automation roadmap. This is absence of evidence from web search; stealth or very early companies may exist.

## 2. What do traditional system integrators and consultancies charge, and how long do automation feasibility studies take? (Incumbent workflow)

### Takeaway
Feasibility and concept studies from integrators or robotics consultants are typically fixed-fee, **about $5K-$50K, over 2-8 weeks**, and are often a loss-leader into a build. Integration itself runs **4-6x the robot's cost**, at $100-$200/hr or $30-50K/month. MBB-style operations or transformation work runs **$400-800K per week per team**, with 8-12 week studies costing $0.5-1.5M. Most of the numbers I found come from secondary or vendor blogs, not from integrators' published price lists, which generally don't exist.

### Cited Findings
- Robotics consultants typically offer feasibility studies as fixed-fee engagements "over 2-6 weeks," delivering "a costed specification and a go or no-go recommendation." Scope covers payback modeling, robot and tooling selection, cell architecture, controls integration, perception and motion software, and safety risk assessment. — [NeuralChain AI blog (secondary)](https://neuralchainai.com/blog/robotics-consulting-services-engagement-models-cost-2/)
- Integrator fees range "from a few thousand to several hundred thousand dollars, equaling an estimated four to six times the initial cost of the robot." — [Inbolt](https://www.inbolt.com/resources/blog/what-are-integration-costs/)
- System integrators charge about $100-$200/hr, and larger engagements are billed at $30K-$50K per month. — [Opsima](https://opsima.com/blog/industry-insights/true-cost-system-integrators/) (secondary)
- General feasibility studies in the US cost $5K for pre-feasibility screening up to $150K+ for lender-grade analysis. Most decision-quality industrial studies land at $15K-$50K. — [Bristol A2 blog (secondary)](https://bristola2.com/feeds/blog/feasibility-study-cost). Feasibility consultants charge $150-$500/hr, and studies run $10K-$100K+ over 2-8 weeks. — [Simple Feasibility](https://www.simplefeasibility.com/blog/feasibility-study-cost)
- Pre-engineered robotic welding cells cost about $130K-$250K (robot, power source, positioner, enclosure, controls). — [Zhouxiang blog (vendor)](https://zxweldingrobot.com/blog/robotic-welding-cell-cost/); see also [Robotiq blog on welding cell cost](https://blog.robotiq.com/bid/72441/What-is-the-Cost-of-a-Robotic-Welding-Cell)
- Financing commentary: the bulk of automation project cost "sits" in integration, not the robot. — [Dimension Funding](https://dimensionfunding.com/automation-project-cost-breakdown)
- MBB operations rate cards run "roughly $400k to $800k per week per team." Public contracts show McKinsey billing about $1,100-$1,200/hr for a senior partner, about $800/hr for an engagement manager and $300-$500/hr for analysts. A typical strategy project costs $500K-$1.5M for 8-12 weeks. — [Management Consulted / RocketBlocks (secondary summaries)](https://managementconsulted.com/top-manufacturing-consulting-firms/); [RocketBlocks](https://rocketblocks.me/guide/business-model.php)
- Accenture now productizes the twin-based redesign workflow (Physical AI Orchestrator; Oct 2025), and Accenture partners with Schaeffler on humanoid automation with NVIDIA and Microsoft (Apr 2025). — [Accenture newsroom](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2025/accenture-launches-physical-ai-orchestrator-to-help-manufacturers-build-software-defined-facilities); [Accenture/Schaeffler](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2025/accenture-and-schaeffler-pave-the-way-for-industrial-humanoid-robots-with-nvidia-and-microsoft-technologies)
- Traditional factory layout planning takes "3-6 months" versus 4-8 weeks with AI generative layout plus twin validation (vendor claim). — [iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/greenfield-consulting/greenfield-smart-factory-design-ai-driven-plant-layout-optimization)

### Inferences
- For a US SMB, the realistic incumbent path is free or cheap integrator pre-sales scoping, biased toward that integrator's capabilities, followed by a paid concept or feasibility study ($5-50K, 2-8 weeks) for **one cell at a time**. A whole-plant dark-factory roadmap is effectively only available from Big-4/MBB or Accenture at six to seven figures. That price gap (roughly $50K to $1M+) is where an AI product priced at about $2-25K per facility plan could sit.
- Because integrators often give scoping away to win builds, a pure-software plan competes with "free." Value must come from neutrality, breadth (whole facility plus supply chain) and speed (days, not months), or the plan must be monetized through downstream integration, financing or equipment referral.

### Gaps
- I found no A3, CSIA or integrator-published (ATS, JR Automation, Bastian) price list for feasibility studies. These figures come from secondary blogs and should be validated by interviewing integrators.
- I found no reliable public data on how often integrators credit study fees toward builds. It is commonly reported anecdotally but unsourced here.
- I found no Deloitte or BCG-specific manufacturing-diagnostic pricing.

## 3. Players in apparel, textile and custom print-on-demand automation

### Takeaway
Apparel automation is a set of point machines and closed microfactory models: Kornit (printing lines), SoftWear Automation (sewing robots), Unspun (3D weaving) and Resonance (on-demand platform plus NYC sewing studio). None offers an AI planning service that designs a POD or apparel shop's end-to-end automated line. That makes the vertical an attractive beachhead, but sewing remains the hardest step to automate.

### Cited Findings
- **Kornit Digital**: the **Apollo** is an all-in-one automated DTG production line (automated loading, printing, curing and unloading) at up to 400 impressions/hr. It is sold under an "All-Inclusive Click" (AIC) usage-based payment model. Monster Digital added 10 Apollo systems in 2025 to move from screen to digital. — [Large Format Review](https://www.largeformatreview.com/hardware/textile-printing/monster-digital-to-add-10-kornit-apollo-systems-during-2025/); [FESPA 2025](https://app.fespa.com/event/fespa-2025/product/UHJvZHVjdF8xMjUzNzI5)

## 4. LLM/agent products for manufacturing engineering, and the gaps they leave

### Takeaway
LLM copilots and agents now exist at almost every **downstream** engineering step: PLC code (Siemens Eigen Engineering Agent, Rockwell Copilot, Copia Copilot), robot code (ABB), CAM programming (CloudNC, Toolpath), MES/frontline apps (Tulip agents, MachineMetrics Max AI) and simulation (Dassault and Siemens companions). They all assume the automation concept already exists and a trained engineer is driving. None does **upstream** "what should we automate, with what equipment, in what layout, in what sequence, at what ROI" for a whole facility.

### Cited Findings
- **Siemens Eigen Engineering Agent** (GA at Hannover Messe, April 2026): a purpose-built AI agent inside TIA Portal that autonomously executes PLC programming, HMI visualization and device configuration with multi-step self-correction. It was piloted with 100+ companies in 19 countries. Claimed results: 2-5x faster execution, up to 50% higher engineering efficiency, up to 80% better solution quality. — [Computer&Automation (EN)](https://www.computer-automation.de/engineering-en/hannover-messe-2026--siemens-presents-new-ai-agent.htm); [Siemens press PDF](https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:49376415-d240-4a3b-934e-6f20d46cfe96/HQCOPR202604167383DE.pdf)

### Inferences
- The engineering value chain runs: opportunity identification, then concept and feasibility, then equipment selection and layout, then detailed design, then controls/robot code, then commissioning, then operations. LLM tooling is now dense at controls/robot code (Siemens, Rockwell, ABB, Copia), machining (CloudNC, Toolpath) and operations (Tulip, MachineMetrics). It is thin to absent at opportunity identification and concept/layout for SMBs. The proposed product sits upstream and could export into these tools: hand off PLC scope to Eigen or Copilot and cells to Vention or Robotiq.
- The incumbents' agents are locked to their ecosystems (TIA Portal, Logix, RobotStudio, 3DEXPERIENCE), so they reinforce the vendor-neutrality white space.

## 6. Where is the clear white space? Is "end-to-end AI automation architect for SMB manufacturers" unoccupied?

### Takeaway
Largely unoccupied as of Oct 2026, but closing quickly. No one sells a **vendor-neutral, whole-facility, multi-modal-input (video, photos, floor plan, ERP, specs) automation roadmap** with equipment specs, layout, integration architecture, phased ROI and supply-chain sourcing to SMBs. The nearest threats are Robotiq IQ (same input-to-design loop, palletizing only, through integrators), Vention (AI cell design, own hardware), RaaS firms (Formic, RobCo) using assessment as pre-sales, and enterprise twins (Siemens, NVIDIA, Accenture, Dassault) that could move down-market.

### Cited Findings
- Robotiq IQ already converts "voice notes, legacy file uploads, 3D site scanning" into "validated workcell designs" with simulation, but only for palletizing and only through integrator partners (June 2026). — [Robotics & Automation News](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2026/06/03/robotiq-launches-ai-platform-to-automate-robotic-workcell-integration/102227/)
- Vention's generative AI designs cells inside its own catalog, and it serves 4,000+ factories at enterprise and mid-market scale. — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/montreal-industrial-ai-scaleup-vention-raises-110-million-usd-series-d/); [MMS](https://www.mmsonline.com/articles/can-ai-automate-automation-)
- RaaS pre-sales assessment is human-led and tied to the provider's fleet (Formic hourly or monthly pricing). — [Plastics Machinery & Mfg](https://www.plasticsmachinerymanufacturing.com/injection-molding/article/53067225/startups-automation-takes-over-potentially-dangerous-job)
- Enterprise twins (Accenture Physical AI Orchestrator, Siemens Digital Twin Composer, NVIDIA Mega) target large manufacturers (PepsiCo, Foxconn, Schaeffler). — [Accenture](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2025/accenture-launches-physical-ai-orchestrator-to-help-manufacturers-build-software-defined-facilities); [eeNews Europe](https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/siemens-unveils-industrial-ai-technologies-at-ces-2026/); [NVIDIA](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2025/NVIDIA-Omniverse-Physical-AI-Operating-System-Expands-to-More-Industries-and-Partners/default.aspx)
- The input layer is being built separately: Vision Lab (YC P2025) structures factory workflows from first-person video plus SOPs. — [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/industry/manufacturing)
- Harmoni frames the SMB target as about 280,000 US factories with traditional machinery. — [Harmoni](https://harmoni.io/blog/this-startup-raised-10-million-to-bring-ai-to-old-factories-2/)
- "Dark factory" is now a widely used term in AI *software* engineering (StrongDM, OpenHands, Pulumi), which muddies search and branding for a manufacturing product. — [OpenHands](https://www.openhands.dev/blog/what-is-an-ai-dark-factory); [Pulumi](https://www.pulumi.com/blog/dark-factory-pattern-pulumi-autonomous-iac/)

### Positioning map (synthesized from the findings above)

| Player | Input to plan? | Scope | Vendor-neutral? | Target | Gap vs concept |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Robotiq IQ (2026) | Yes (voice, files, 3D scan) | Single cell (palletizing) | No (Robotiq) | Integrators, SMB/mid | Single application, no facility roadmap, no ERP/supply chain |
| Vention MachineBuilder AI | Partial (user designs, AI assists) | Cell/machine | No (Vention parts) | Mid/enterprise, 4,000+ factories | User must know what to build; no opportunity discovery |
| Formic / RobCo (RaaS) | Human assessment | Cell | No (own fleet) | US SMB | Narrow apps; no plan product |
| Bright Machines | Internal | Electronics assembly line | No | Electronics OEMs/EMS | Vertical, own hardware |
| Siemens DT Composer / Eigen | Expert CAD/twin inputs | Plant / controls | Siemens stack | Enterprise | Expert-operated, costly, downstream |
| NVIDIA Mega / Accenture PAIO | Expert inputs | Plant / fleet sim | Partially (Omniverse) | Enterprise | Consulting-led, 6-7 figures |
| Dassault Virtual Companions | Platform data | Product to factory | 3DS stack | Enterprise | Platform lock-in |
| Rockwell/ABB/Copia copilots | Code/specs | Controls/robot code | Own stack | Engineers | Downstream only |
| Integrators / MBB | Human site visits | Cell (SI) to plant (MBB) | SI-biased / neutral | All / enterprise | Slow (weeks to months), costly ($5-50K per cell; $0.5-1.5M MBB) |

### Inferences
- **White space 1: opportunity discovery and prioritization from messy SMB data.** Taking video, photos, floor plans and ERP routing/volume data and producing "these 7 processes, in this order, with this payback." Nobody productizes this for SMBs.
- **White space 2: vendor-neutral equipment selection and multi-vendor layout** across robots, conveyors, AMRs, vision and printers/CNCs, with specs and BOMs, instead of a single vendor's catalog.
- **White space 3: phased "dark-factory roadmap,"** meaning the staged path from current state to lights-out (cells, then lines, then material flow, then MES/controls, then unattended shifts), including capex versus RaaS/click financing and integrator RFP packages. Only MBB/Accenture-type engagements do this today, at enterprise prices.
- **White space 4: vertical depth in under-served SMB niches** such as POD apparel, job-shop machining and food co-packing. Equipment vendors (Kornit, SoftWear) sell machines, not plans.
- **Risks:** (a) Robotiq IQ or Vention extending from single-cell to multi-application planning; (b) RaaS players adding AI assessment as a free funnel; (c) Siemens/NVIDIA/Accenture packaging SMB templates (Siemens is already putting Composer on the Xcelerator Marketplace); (d) integrators giving scoping away for free; (e) liability and accuracy, since a plan without site validation may be distrusted, so the product likely needs a human-engineer-in-the-loop or an integrator network (Robotiq's "amplify partners" framing suggests the market expects this).
- **Business model implication:** a standalone plan fee competes with "free" pre-sales, so monetizing through downstream referral or integration marketplace fees, financing or RaaS origination, or equipment sourcing margin may be necessary. That would align the product with how Formic, Vention and Robotiq actually make money.

### Gaps
- I found no market-sizing data specific to "automation planning for SMBs" (number of US SMB manufacturers actively evaluating automation, or willingness to pay for plans).
- Stealth or very early startups (pre-seed, non-YC) in "AI automation architect" may exist and not be indexed. A targeted Crunchbase/PitchBook query would close this.
- I did not verify whether A3 or CSIA provide integrator-matching services that a planner could plug into.
